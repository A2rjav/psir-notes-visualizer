### The Death of Détente and the Outbreak of the "Second Cold War" (1979–1985)
- **Archival Reference:** `page_146.png` to `page_148.png` (Afghan Invasion & Second Cold War Breakdown).
- **The Catalyst: Soviet Invasion of Afghanistan (December 1979):**
  - Soviet tanks rolled into Kabul to prop up a faltering Marxist regime, provoking universal Western outrage.
  - President Jimmy Carter declared the **Carter Doctrine** (any attempt by an outside force to gain control of the Persian Gulf will be regarded as an assault on vital US interests) and boycotted the 1980 Moscow Olympics.
  - Détente was definitively buried; the US Senate refused to ratify SALT II.
- **Ronald Reagan and the Offensive Pivot (1981):**
  - Elected on an uncompromising anti-communist platform, Ronald Reagan characterized the USSR as the **"Evil Empire"** and an aberration of history.
  - Abandoned containment in favor of **"Rollback"** and peace through strength:
    - Massively increased US defense spending (surpassing \$1.5 trillion over five years).
    - Deployed Pershing II and Tomahawk cruise missiles in Western Europe to counter Soviet SS-20s.
    - Launched the **Reagan Doctrine**: covertly funding anti-communist insurgencies worldwide (Afghan Mujahideen, Nicaraguan Contras, UNITA in Angola).
- **The Strategic Defense Initiative (SDI - "Star Wars", 1983):**
  - Reagan proposed developing space-based directed-energy weapons and satellite lasers capable of intercepting incoming Soviet ballistic missiles.
  - Shattered the foundation of the 1972 ABM Treaty and terrified Soviet leadership, who realized the technologically and economically stagnating Soviet economy could not match a space-based arms race.

### Mikhail Gorbachev's "New Political Thinking"
- **Archival Reference:** `page_148.png` to `page_150.png` (Perestroika, Glasnost & Novoye Myshleniye).
- **Mikhail Gorbachev's Ascent (March 1985):** Recognized that the USSR was in acute structural crisis — 25% of Soviet GDP was consumed by the military-industrial complex, consumer goods were scarce, agriculture had collapsed, and technological innovation had ground to a halt.
- **Three Pillars of Gorbachev's Domestic and International Reform:**
  - *1. Perestroika (Economic Restructuring):* Decentralizing state planning, introducing limited private enterprise incentives, and modernizing industrial production.
  - *2. Glasnost (Political Openness):* Lifting state censorship, permitting public criticism of Stalinist atrocities, and encouraging civic debate.
  - *3. Novoye Myshleniye (New Political Thinking in Foreign Policy):*
    - Repudiated the Marxist-Leninist dogma of inevitable class war between capitalism and socialism.
    - Asserted that in the nuclear age, **Security is Common and Indivisible**; states cannot achieve security at the expense of their adversary.
    - Subordinated class struggle to universal human values, global ecological survival, and mutual disarmament.

### Historic Disarmament: Geneva to the INF Treaty (1987)
- **Archival Reference:** `page_150.png` to `page_152.png` (Summit Diplomacy & Eliminating Nuclear Classes).
- **The Reagan-Gorbachev Summits:**
  - *Geneva (1985) & Reykjavik (1986):* Groundbreaking personal chemistry; at Reykjavik, both leaders came astonishingly close to discussing the total abolition of all ballistic nuclear weapons.
- **The Intermediate-Range Nuclear Forces (INF) Treaty (December 1987):**
  - Signed in Washington; marked the **first time in history that superpowers agreed to eliminate an entire class of nuclear delivery vehicles** (land-based missiles with ranges of 500 to 5,500 km).
  - Destroyed 2,692 operational missiles and introduced intrusive, revolutionary on-site verification inspections.
- **Conventional Forces in Europe (CFE) Treaty (1990) & START I (1991):**
  - Deep cuts in conventional armaments and strategic nuclear warheads, winding down four decades of hair-trigger nuclear alert.

### The Sinatra Doctrine and the Unraveling of the Eastern Bloc (1989)
- **Archival Reference:** `page_152.png` to `page_154.png` (Fall of the Wall & Velvet Revolutions).
- **Repudiation of the Brezhnev Doctrine:**
  - In 1968, the Brezhnev Doctrine justified Soviet military intervention to preserve communist rule in any Warsaw Pact state (e.g., crushing the Prague Spring).
  - Gorbachev announced the **"Sinatra Doctrine"** (jokingly referencing Frank Sinatra's song *"My Way"*) — the USSR would no longer use military force to keep Eastern European communist regimes in power; each country could chart its own political path.
- **The Cascade of 1989 (The Autumn of Nations):**
  - *Poland:* Free elections won by Lech Wałęsa's Solidarity movement.
  - *Hungary:* Dismantled its border fence with Austria, opening the iron curtain.
  - *East Germany:* On November 9, 1989, civilian crowds breached and demolished the **Berlin Wall**, reunifying Germany within NATO in 1990.
  - *Czechoslovakia:* The peaceful **Velvet Revolution** elected dissident playwright Václav Havel as president.
  - *Romania:* The violent overthrow and execution of dictator Nicolae Ceaușescu.

### The Collapse of the Soviet Union (1991): Internal vs External Causes
- **Archival Reference:** `page_154.png` to `page_155.png` (August Coup, Belovezha Accords & Disintegration).
- **The Collapse:**
  - Hardline communist attempt to overthrow Gorbachev in the failed August 1981 Coup.
  - Boris Yeltsin, President of the Russian Federation, banned the Communist Party.
  - On December 25, 1991, the red Soviet hammer-and-sickle flag was lowered over the Kremlin; the USSR dissolved into 15 independent sovereign republics.
- **Scholarly Debate on Why the USSR Collapsed:**
  - *Triumphalist School (Peter Schweizer, Reagan Conservatives):* Reagan's military buildup, SDI, and the Reagan Doctrine economically bankrupted and broke the Soviet leadership.
  - *Internal Reform Failure School (Stephen Cohen, Archie Brown):* Gorbachev's reforms were fatally missequenced — releasing political freedoms (Glasnost) before fixing the command economy (Perestroika) unleashed suppressed ethnic nationalism that fractured the union.
  - *Material Exhaustion School (Gaddis):* The structural bankruptcy of central command planning, imperial overstretch in Eastern Europe and Afghanistan, and loss of ideological legitimacy.

### Active Recall Flashcards (Batch 14)
- **Q1:** What was the significance of the 1987 Intermediate-Range Nuclear Forces (INF) Treaty?
  **A1:** It was the first nuclear arms treaty in history to eliminate an entire category of nuclear weapons (land-based missiles of 500–5,500 km range) with unprecedented on-site verification.
- **Q2:** How did Gorbachev's "Sinatra Doctrine" dismantle the Soviet bloc in Eastern Europe?
  **A2:** It overturned the Brezhnev Doctrine by declaring that the USSR would no longer intervene militarily to prop up Eastern European communist regimes, allowing them to democratize in 1989.
- **Q3:** What were the two core domestic pillars of Mikhail Gorbachev's reform agenda?
  **A3:** Perestroika (structural economic restructuring) and Glasnost (political openness and freedom of speech).

### Model Answer: The Disintegration of the Soviet Union (20 Marks)
- **Question:** "Was the collapse of the Soviet Union a triumph of American grand strategy or the inevitable consequence of internal structural contradictions?" Critically evaluate.
- **Structure:**
  - **Introduction:** Outline the monumental significance of December 1991 as the end of the Cold War and the Westphalian transition to a unipolar international system.
  - **Body Paragraph 1:** Examine the American Triumphalist argument: Reagan's defense modernization, Star Wars (SDI), the Reagan Doctrine in Afghanistan, and technological embargoes pushing the Soviet state over the fiscal cliff.
  - **Body Paragraph 2:** Analyze the Internalist economic critique: systemic failures of central Gosplan planning, pervasive corruption, agricultural paralysis, and economic distortion from spending 25% of GDP on the defense sector.
  - **Body Paragraph 3:** Evaluate the role of ideas and leadership: Gorbachev's "New Political Thinking," the unintended consequences of Glasnost unleashing long-suppressed peripheral ethno-nationalism in the Baltics, Ukraine, and Caucasus.
  - **Critical Evaluation:** Synthesize that while Reagan's pressure accelerated the crisis, a system possessing 40,000 nuclear warheads could only collapse without catastrophic war because its own ruling elite lost faith in its legitimizing Marxist ideology.
  - **Conclusion:** Conclude that the Soviet demise was a confluence of structural bankruptcy, imperial overstretch (Kennedy's thesis), and transformative leadership choices, rather than an unalloyed military victory for Washington.
