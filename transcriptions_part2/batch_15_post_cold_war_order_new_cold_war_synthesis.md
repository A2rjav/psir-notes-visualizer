### The Unipolar Moment and Liberal Triumphalism
- **Archival Reference:** `page_156.png` to `page_158.png` (The End of History & Unipolar Hegemony).
- **Charles Krauthammer's "The Unipolar Moment" (Foreign Affairs, 1990/91):**
  - Following the Soviet collapse, the world was no longer bipolar, nor was it multipolar.
  - The international system entered a historically unique period of **Unipolarity** centered on the undisputed geopolitical, economic, and military preponderance of the United States.
- **Francis Fukuyama's *The End of History and the Last Man* (1992):**
  - Drawing on Hegel and Alexandre Kojève, Fukuyama argued that humanity's ideological evolution had reached its final terminus.
  - Fascism, Nazism, and Communism had been decisively defeated. Western liberal democracy and market capitalism emerged as the **"final form of human government"**.
- **John Ikenberry's *Liberal Leviathan* (2011):**
  - The US established a rule-based international order characterized by open markets, multilateral institutions (WTO, IMF, World Bank), and democratic security pacts. Other states consented to American hegemony because it was institutionalized, predictable, and provided global public goods (freedom of navigation).

### Fracturing of the Liberal Order: Realist & Civilizational Critiques
- **Archival Reference:** `page_158.png` to `page_160.png` (Clash of Civilizations & Imperial Overstretch).
- **Samuel P. Huntington's *The Clash of Civilizations* (1993/1996):**
  - Direct refutation of Fukuyama's universal liberal convergence.
  - Post-Cold War conflicts would not be primarily ideological or economic, but cultural and religious: *"The fault lines between civilizations will be the battle lines of the future."*
  - Identified major civilizational blocs (Western, Islamic, Sinic/Confucian, Hindu, Orthodox Slavic, etc.) and warned of conflicts along the civilizational "bloody borders" of Islam and the West, and a potential Confucian-Islamic alliance.
- **Realist Critiques (Mearsheimer & Waltz):**
  - The "Unipolar Moment" was a fleeting geopolitical anomaly. Anarchy inevitably compels other states to balance against an unrestrained hegemon.
  - Mearsheimer's *The Tragedy of Great Power Politics* predicted that American attempts to spread democracy through military intervention (Iraq, Libya) represented delusional liberal imperial hubris that would end in catastrophe and accelerate American imperial overstretch.

### The Return of Geopolitics and Revisionist Great Powers
- **Archival Reference:** `page_160.png` to `page_162.png` (Russia, China & Challenging the Status Quo).
- **Russian Revanchism and NATO Expansion:**
  - Russia under Vladimir Putin viewed NATO's post-Cold War eastward expansion (absorbing Poland, Baltics, Romania) as a profound Western betrayal and existential encirclement.
  - 2007 Munich Security Conference speech: Putin declared unipolarity unacceptable and illegitimate.
  - *Strategic Flashpoints:* Russo-Georgian War (2008), Annexation of Crimea (2014), and the full-scale invasion of Ukraine (2022) marked the violent end of the post-Cold War European security architecture.
- **The Rise of China and the Thucydides' Trap:**
  - China's exponential economic, technological, and military ascent under Xi Jinping transitioned Beijing from Deng Xiaoping's low-profile posture (*Taoguang Yanghui*) to assertive great-power rivalry.
  - *Graham Allison's Thucydides' Trap:* When a rising power (China) threatens to displace an established ruling power (USA), structural stress makes violent conflict historically probable.
  - *Manifestations:* Militarization of the South China Sea, Belt and Road Initiative (BRI), Taiwan straits tensions, and the "no-limits" partnership with Moscow.

### Dmitri Trenin's Thesis: The Advent of "Cold War 2.0"
- **Archival Reference:** `page_162.png` to `page_163.png` (Cold War 1.0 vs Cold War 2.0 Compared).
- **Cold War 2.0 Conceptual Framework (Dmitri Trenin):**
  - Geopolitical confrontation has returned between the West and a revisionist Eurasian axis (China-Russia-Iran-North Korea).
- **Key Distinctions between Cold War 1.0 and Cold War 2.0:**
  1. *Ideology:* Cold War 1.0 was a universal ideological crusade between Communism and Capitalism. Cold War 2.0 is a pragmatic contest between Western-led rules versus national sovereignty, civilizational autonomy, and multipolar balance.
  2. *Economic Interdependence:* In Cold War 1.0, superpower economies were entirely segregated. In Cold War 2.0, the US, EU, and China are deeply entwined through global supply chains, leading to complex strategies of **"De-risking"** rather than total decoupling.
  3. *Polarity:* Cold War 1.0 was strictly bipolar. Cold War 2.0 unfolds in a messy, polycentric world where powerful middle powers and the Global South (India, Brazil, Saudi Arabia, South Africa, Turkey) refuse to join binary blocs.
  4. *Battlegrounds:* Shifted from the Fulda Gap in Europe to the **Indo-Pacific maritime commons**, artificial intelligence, semiconductor lithography, quantum computing, and critical minerals.

### S. Jaishankar's Indian Worldview: Multipolarity & Strategic Multi-Alignment
- **Archival Reference:** `page_163.png` to `page_164.png` (The India Way & Volume Synthesis).
- **External Affairs Minister S. Jaishankar's *The India Way: Strategies for an Uncertain World* (2020):**
  - India is navigating a world in transition from an exhausted unipolar order to an as-yet-undefined multipolar reality.
  - **Rejection of Cold War Dogmas:** India has transcended both the ideological non-alignment of the Cold War and the temptation of formal alliance treaties.
  - **Issue-Based Strategic Multi-Alignment:**
    - India engages multiple competing poles simultaneously based on enlightened national interest.
    - Member of the **Quad** (with US, Japan, Australia) to preserve a free, open, and rules-based Indo-Pacific against Chinese maritime hegemony.
    - Concurrently member of **BRICS** and the **Shanghai Cooperation Organisation (SCO)** to preserve Eurasian connectivity and reform global financial governance.
  - **India as a "Leading Power" & "Vishwa Mitra":** Transitioning from a rule-taker to a rule-shaper; serving as a bridge between the developed North and the developing Global South.

### Active Recall Flashcards (Batch 15)
- **Q1:** What was Charles Krauthammer's definition of the "Unipolar Moment"?
  **A1:** The unique post-Cold War geopolitical era characterized by the uncontested military, technological, and economic supremacy of the United States as the sole global superpower.
- **Q2:** How does Cold War 2.0 differ economically from the historical Cold War?
  **A2:** Cold War 1.0 featured completely isolated, decoupled economic blocs; Cold War 2.0 features deep economic and technological entanglement between the US, Europe, and China, requiring strategies of "de-risking" rather than outright separation.
- **Q3:** How does S. Jaishankar conceptualize India's strategy of "Multi-Alignment"?
  **A3:** Rather than joining rigid alliances, India maintains issue-based partnerships across rival power centers (e.g., Quad for maritime security; BRICS/SCO for multipolarity) to maximize national strategic autonomy.
