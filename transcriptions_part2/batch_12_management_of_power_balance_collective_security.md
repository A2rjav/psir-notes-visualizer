### The Problem of Order in Systemic Anarchy
- **Archival Reference:** `page_125.png` to `page_127.png` (Systemic Anarchy & Mechanisms of Order).
- **The Core Paradox of International Relations:** Because the international system is anarchical (lacking a world sovereign), unchecked power breeds existential threat and imperial conquest.
- **Two Classical Paradigms for Managing Power:**
  1. *Balance of Power (Realist Model):* Decentralized, competitive, self-regulating equilibrium among sovereign states.
  2. *Collective Security (Liberal Institutionalist Model):* Centralized, universal legal commitment where all states unite to punish any aggressor.
- **Inis Claude's Classification (*Power and International Relations*, 1962):** Categorized power management into three conceptual systems: Balance of Power, Collective Security, and World Government.

### The Balance of Power (BoP): Theory, Techniques & Operation
- **Archival Reference:** `page_127.png` to `page_130.png` (Balance of Power Mechanics & Classical Rules).
- **Core Meaning:** A distribution of power among nations such that no single state or coalition is permitted to accumulate preponderant strength capable of imposing its will or establishing a universal hegemony.
- **Underlying Realist Logic:** "Power can only be checked by counter-power."
- **Classic Techniques of Balancing Power:**
  - *1. Internal Balancing:* Enhancing domestic military capabilities, technological innovation, economic growth, and industrial mobilization (the primary technique identified by Kenneth Waltz in neorealism).
  - *2. External Balancing (Alliances and Counter-Alliances):* Forming defensive pacts to pool military capabilities against a common hegemon (e.g., Quad, AUKUS, NATO).
  - *3. Buffer States:* Creating or preserving weak neutral states between rival empires to prevent direct military friction (e.g., historical Afghanistan between British India and Tsarist Russia; Nepal/Tibet between India and China).
  - *4. Partition and Compensation:* Great powers mutually carving up disputed territories so that none gains a relative advantage over the other (e.g., historical partitions of Poland).
  - *5. Armaments and Disarmament:* Calibrating weapons stockpiles to maintain strategic parity.
  - *6. The Role of the "Holder of the Balance" (The Balancer):* A detached maritime power that throws its decisive weight behind the weaker side to preserve systemic equilibrium (historically performed by Great Britain in European continental affairs).

### Critiques of Balance of Power
- **Archival Reference:** `page_130.png` to `page_131.png` (Wilsonian & Non-Aligned Critiques).
- **Woodrow Wilson's Rejection:** In his 1918 Fourteen Points, Wilson condemned Balance of Power as a cynical, war-prone conspiracy of imperial monarchies that inevitably caused World War I. He advocated replacing secret balance-of-power treaties with an open, universal League of Nations.
- **Jawaharlal Nehru's Philosophical Denunciation:**
  - Nehru rejected cold war balance-of-power alliances (NATO, SEATO, CENTO), calling BoP a *"nervous state of peace"* that perpetually fuels mutual suspicion and arms races.
  - Non-Alignment was India's conscious refusal to be drawn into the balance-of-power calculus of superpower blocs.
- **Realist Defense (Waltz & Morgenthau):** Balance of Power does not guarantee perpetual peace; its objective is to **preserve the multi-state system and state sovereignty** by preventing imperial conquest.

### The Collective Security Model: "One for All, All for One"
- **Archival Reference:** `page_131.png` to `page_134.png` (Collective Security Architecture & Inherent Paradoxes).
- **Definition:** An institutionalized security arrangement where every member state agrees that an attack on any single member is considered an attack on all members, triggering an automatic, collective military response.
- **Distinction from Collective Defense:**
  - *Collective Defense (e.g., NATO Article 5):* Directed against a specific, external adversary defined in advance.
  - *Collective Security (e.g., UN Charter):* Universal, non-directional; directed inward against *any* aggressor, even if an erstwhile ally.
- **Six Essential Assumptions of Collective Security (Inis Claude):**
  1. All states must view peace as indivisible.
  2. Aggression must be universally identifiable and universally condemned.
  3. All states must be willing to subordinate their narrow national interests to collective enforcement.
  4. The collective strength of the international community must be overwhelmingly superior to any potential aggressor.
  5. States must possess sufficient trust and political solidarity to act in concert.
  6. States must possess military flexibility to deploy forces rapidly anywhere on the globe.
- **Structural Failures in Practice:**
  - *League of Nations (1920–1939):* Complete failure during the Japanese invasion of Manchuria (1931) and Italian invasion of Abyssinia (1935) due to national egoism, appeasement, and US absence.
  - *United Nations Charter Chapter VII:* Paralyzed during the Cold War and contemporary conflicts by the Big-Five **P-5 Veto Power** in the Security Council (Article 27). The system is legally incapable of acting when aggression is committed or sponsored by a permanent member.

### Active Recall Flashcards (Batch 12)
- **Q1:** What is the critical distinction between Internal Balancing and External Balancing?
  **A1:** Internal balancing relies on domestic economic expansion and indigenous military modernization; external balancing relies on forming diplomatic coalitions, alliances, and pacts with other states.
- **Q2:** How did Inis Claude define the foundational ethos of Collective Security?
  **A2:** Collective security operates on the principle of "One for All and All for One" — an attack on any one state is treated as an attack on all members of the international community.
- **Q3:** Why does the UN Charter Chapter VII collective security mechanism routinely face paralysis?
  **A3:** Because Article 27 grants absolute veto power to the five permanent members (P-5), preventing enforcement whenever a P-5 member or its vital proxy is involved in conflict.

### Model Answer: Balance of Power versus Collective Security (20 Marks)
- **Question:** "Collective Security was conceived as a superior liberal alternative to the Machiavellian Balance of Power, yet in practice, it has repeatedly fallen victim to the very power politics it sought to abolish." Critically discuss.
- **Structure:**
  - **Introduction:** Contrast the Hobbesian realist foundation of Balance of Power with the Kantian liberal ideal of Collective Security as competing approaches to managing systemic power.
  - **Body Paragraph 1:** Elaborate the Balance of Power doctrine, detailing its classical mechanisms (internal/external balancing, buffer zones, role of the balancer) and its defense of the Westphalian multi-state system.
  - **Body Paragraph 2:** Examine the philosophical foundations of Collective Security (indivisibility of peace, institutionalized law enforcement) and its institutionalization in the League of Nations and UN Charter Chapter VII.
  - **Body Paragraph 3:** Critically evaluate the structural contradictions of Collective Security: reliance on idealist solidarity, selective enforcement, free-rider dilemmas, and paralysis by great power vetoes.
  - **Synthesis with Contemporary Realities:** Show how the global order has defaulted back to regional balance of power arrangements (NATO expansion, Indo-Pacific minilaterals like Quad/AUKUS) amidst the collective security paralysis of the UN Security Council.
  - **Conclusion:** Reaffirm Inis Claude's insight that collective security requires the very preconditions of harmony that, if they existed, would make international security mechanisms redundant in the first place.
