### Kenneth Waltz and the Scientific Revolution: *Theory of International Politics* (1979)
- **Archival Reference:** `page_069.png` to `page_072.png` (Shift from Human Nature to Structure).
- **The Intellectual Breakthrough:** Kenneth Waltz revolutionized international relations theory by discarding Morgenthau's subjective, unpredictable reliance on "human nature" and creating a rigorous, deductive, scientific theory: **Structural Realism (Neo-Realism)**.
- **The Three Images of International Relations (*Man, the State and War*, 1959):**
  1. *First Image (The Individual):* Explains war through human nature, egoism, and individual psychology (Morgenthau, Niebuhr).
  2. *Second Image (The State):* Explains war through internal domestic regime types (e.g., Lenin blaming capitalist imperialism, liberals blaming autocracies).
  3. *Third Image (The International System):* Explains war through the **anarchic structure of the international system**. Waltz established that the Third Image is the permissive, structural cause of all international conflict.
- **The Three Components of International Political Structure:**
  1. *Ordering Principle:* Systemic **Anarchy** (decentralized, absence of overarching sovereign authority; domestic politics is hierarchical, international politics is anarchic).
  2. *Character / Differentiation of Units:* **Functional Like-Units**. All states perform identical core sovereign functions (defense, taxation, governance) to survive. There is no functional differentiation among states.
  3. *Distribution of Capabilities:* States differ radically only in their **relative material power and military capabilities**. The distribution of capabilities defines the polarity of the international system (Unipolar, Bipolar, Multipolar).

### Bipolarity vs Multipolarity: Waltz's Defense of Bipolar Stability
- **Archival Reference:** `page_072.png` to `page_074.png` (Polarity & Systemic Stability).
- **Waltz's Counter-Intuitive Thesis:** Bipolar systems (e.g., the Cold War US-Soviet standoff) are **inherently more stable and peaceful** than multipolar systems (e.g., pre-WWI European balance of power).
- **Why Bipolarity is More Stable:**
  1. *Clarity of Threats:* Only two superpowers exist; each knows who the enemy is. There is zero ambiguity about where the primary threat originates.
  2. *Absence of Chain-Ganging:* In multipolar systems, great powers are dragged into unwanted catastrophic wars by weak allies ("Chain-Ganging", e.g., Germany dragged into WWI by Austria-Hungary). In a bipolar system, superpowers dominate their allies and cannot be trapped by them.
  3. *Internal Balancing over External Alliances:* Superpowers rely primarily on their own domestic economic and military mobilization (**Internal Balancing**), rather than fickle, shifting foreign coalitions (**External Balancing**).
  4. *Low Risk of Miscalculation:* Bipolarity drastically minimizes the danger of great power miscalculation, whereas multipolar systems generate structural miscalculations leading to war.

### Defensive Realism: Waltz and Robert Jervis
- **Archival Reference:** `page_074.png` to `page_076.png` (Defensive Realism & Security Dilemma).
- **Core Axiom of Defensive Realism:** Under anarchy, states seek **Security**, not power for its own sake. States are status-quo seekers whose primary goal is **Survival**.
- **The Security Dilemma (John Herz, Robert Jervis):**
  - A condition where the defensive actions taken by State A to increase its own security (e.g., purchasing defensive missiles, building forts) are perceived by State B as inherently threatening and offensive.
  - State B reacts by arming itself; State A perceives State B's reaction as aggressive, triggering a spiral of mutual mistrust and an arms race where both states end up less secure than before.
- **Why Hegemony is Counter-Productive:** Seeking universal dominance or excessive power is self-defeating. The international system naturally generates a **Balancing Coalition** against any state that attempts to achieve hegemony (e.g., Napoleonic France, Imperial Germany, Nazi Germany were all crushed by balancing coalitions). Therefore, wise states seek an "appropriate amount of power," not hegemony.

### Offensive Realism: John Mearsheimer's Tragedy of Great Power Politics
- **Archival Reference:** `page_076.png` to `page_079.png` (Offensive Realism & The Drive for Hegemony).
- **John Mearsheimer (*The Tragedy of Great Power Politics*, 2001):** The leading exponent of Offensive Realism, directly refuting Waltz's defensive complacency.
- **Five Bedrock Assumptions of Offensive Realism:**
  1. The international system is anarchic.
  2. Great powers inherently possess offensive military capabilities to hurt or destroy each other.
  3. States can never be certain about other states' future intentions (benign intentions today can turn aggressive tomorrow).
  4. Survival is the primary driving goal of great powers.
  5. Great powers are rational actors calculating consequences.
- **The Inescapable Logic of Offensive Realism:**
  - Because states are gripped by perpetual fear and can never know how much power is "enough" to guarantee survival in an uncertain future, **they are compelled to maximize their share of global power**.
  - The ultimate guarantee of survival is to become the **Hegemon**. The only secure state is the most powerful state in the system.
  - States do not seek to preserve the status quo; they are structural revisionists seeking every opportunity to alter the balance of power in their favor.
- **Regional Hegemony vs Global Hegemony:**
  - True global hegemony is an impossibility because of the **"Stopping Power of Water"** (oceans make projecting overwhelming conquering force across continents extremely difficult).
  - Therefore, great powers seek **Regional Hegemony** in their own backyard (as the US achieved in the Americas via the Monroe Doctrine), while acting as an **Offshore Balancer** to prevent any rival power from dominating another region (e.g., preventing China from dominating Asia or Germany from dominating Europe).

### Active Recall Flashcards (Batch 7)
- **Q1:** What are the three structural components of the international political system in Kenneth Waltz's theory?
  **A1:** The Ordering Principle (Anarchy), the Character of the Units (Functional Like-Units), and the Distribution of Capabilities (Polarity: Unipolar, Bipolar, Multipolar).
- **Q2:** Why does Kenneth Waltz argue that Bipolarity is structurally more stable than Multipolarity?
  **A2:** Bipolarity provides clear threat perception, eliminates dangerous alliance chain-ganging, relies on internal rather than external balancing, and drastically reduces the risk of strategic miscalculation.
- **Q3:** How does John Mearsheimer's Offensive Realism differ fundamentally from Waltz's Defensive Realism?
  **A3:** Defensive realists argue states seek only enough power to survive and maintain the status quo; Offensive realists argue that anarchy forces rational states to relentlessly maximize relative power and seek regional hegemony.
