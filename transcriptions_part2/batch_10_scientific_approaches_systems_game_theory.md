### The Second Great Debate: Traditionalism vs Behaviouralism / Scientism
- **Archival Reference:** `page_103.png` to `page_105.png` (Hedley Bull vs Morton Kaplan Debate).
- **The Traditionalist Position (Hedley Bull, Martin Wight):** Argued that international relations is an art, not a physical science. It can only be understood through history, law, philosophy, and diplomatic intuition. Quantitative models and abstract mathematical formulas distort the human reality of politics.
- **The Behavioural / Scientific Position (Morton Kaplan, J. David Singer):** Argued that intuitive traditionalism is unscientific and subjective. IR must adopt the scientific method: rigorous hypothesis testing, quantitative data collection (e.g., Correlates of War project), formal modeling, and predictive systems theory.

### Morton Kaplan's Six Heuristic International System Models
- **Archival Reference:** `page_105.png` to `page_108.png` (System and Process in International Politics, 1957).
- **Kaplan's Methodological Goal:** Constructing abstract heuristic models of international systems governed by specific operational behavioral rules, analyzing conditions under which they maintain equilibrium or transform into another system.
- **Kaplan's Six System Models:**
  1. *The Balance of Power System (Historical 18th–19th Century Europe):*
     - Requires at least five major great powers of comparable capability.
     - *Essential Rules:* Increase capabilities through negotiation rather than war; fight rather than fail to increase capabilities; stop fighting rather than eliminate an essential national actor; oppose any actor seeking hegemony; permit defeated essential actors to re-enter the system.
  2. *The Loose Bipolar System (Cold War Era):*
     - Two dominant superpowers leading rival military blocs (NATO and Warsaw Pact).
     - Presence of non-aligned nations (NAM) and a universal organization (the United Nations) acting as institutional mediators to dampen crises.
  3. *The Tight Bipolar System (Hypothetical Cold War Escalation):*
     - All sovereign states are forcibly integrated into one of the two competing superpower blocs; no non-aligned actors or effective universal organizations exist. Highly unstable and prone to total war.
  4. *The Universal International System (Confederal / UN-centric):*
     - A powerful supranational international organization (e.g., a strengthened UN) possesses sufficient authority and police force to prevent interstate war and enforce universal human rights.
  5. *The Hierarchical System (Imperial / World Government):*
     - A single global empire or world state integrates all nations under one centralized political authority (either through democratic federation or authoritarian conquest).
  6. *The Unit Veto System (Total Nuclear Proliferation):*
     - All sovereign states possess credible, invulnerable, second-strike nuclear weapons capabilities.
     - An extreme "Hobbesian state of nature" in international politics where every state can destroy every other state, creating a frozen equilibrium of universal deterrence and terror.

### Strategic Game Theory in International Relations
- **Archival Reference:** `page_108.png` to `page_111.png` (Rational Choice & Strategic Game Models).
- **Definition of Game Theory:** A formal mathematical framework for analyzing strategic interactions between two or more rational decision-makers where the outcome for each depends on the interdependent choices of all participants.
- **1. The Prisoner's Dilemma (Structural Barrier to Cooperation):**
  - Two criminal suspects are interrogated separately. Each has two choices: **Cooperate** (stay silent) or **Defect** (confess and betray partner).
  - *Payoff Structure:* Mutual defection is worse than mutual cooperation. However, individually, defection is the **Dominant Strategy** because it maximizes personal payoff regardless of what the other does.
  - *IR Application:* Arms races (e.g., India-Pakistan or US-Soviet nuclear buildup). Both nations would be safer and richer by disarming (mutual cooperation), but fear of unilateral defection compels both to continuously arm themselves (sub-optimal Nash equilibrium).
  - *Overcoming the Dilemma (Robert Axelrod's The Evolution of Cooperation, 1984):* Under iterated (repeated) play with an infinite time horizon ("The Shadow of the Future"), the simple **"Tit-for-Tat" Strategy** (cooperate first, then mirror the opponent's previous move) naturally produces sustained cooperation.
- **2. The Game of Chicken (Brinkmanship and Nuclear Crises):**
  - Two drivers race cars directly toward each other on a narrow road. The one who swerves first is the "Chicken" (humiliated); if neither swerves, both die in a head-on collision.
  - *IR Application:* The **Cuban Missile Crisis (1962)**.
  - *Strategic Tactic (Thomas Schelling's The Strategy of Conflict, 1960):* **"The Rationality of Irrationality"** — a player achieves victory by convincingly demonstrating irrationality or deliberately throwing the steering wheel out the window (binding oneself), leaving the other player with no choice but to swerve to avoid mutual destruction.

### Graham Allison's Decision-Making Models: *Essence of Decision* (1971)
- **Archival Reference:** `page_111.png` to `page_113.png` (Three Models of Foreign Policy Analysis).
- **Graham Allison's Breakthrough Study:** Analyzed the 13 days of the October 1962 Cuban Missile Crisis through three distinct conceptual lenses:
- **Model I: The Rational Actor Model (RAM - Classical View):**
  - Views the state as a single, unitary, monolithic rational decision-maker calculating costs and benefits to select the optimal policy that maximizes national interest.
- **Model II: The Organizational Behavior Model:**
  - Foreign policy is not a master calculation, but the output of large, complex governmental organizations functioning according to rigid **Standard Operating Procedures (SOPs)** and established routines (e.g., the Soviet military building missile sites in Cuba using standard, undisguised patterns that US U-2 spy planes easily detected).
- **Model III: The Bureaucratic Politics Model ("Where you sit determines where you stand"):**
  - Foreign policy decisions are the messy outcome of internal bureaucratic bargaining, personal rivalries, power struggles, and compromises among key political and military leaders:
  - *"Where you stand depends on where you sit."* (Defense chiefs lobby for air strikes; diplomats lobby for blockades).

### Active Recall Flashcards (Batch 10)
- **Q1:** What are the six international system models formulated by Morton Kaplan?
  **A1:** Balance of Power; Loose Bipolar; Tight Bipolar; Universal International; Hierarchical; and Unit Veto systems.
- **Q2:** How does Game Theory explain arms races through the Prisoner's Dilemma?
  **A2:** Mutual cooperation (disarmament) is collectively optimal, but because states fear unilateral betrayal under anarchy, rational self-interest drives both to defect (arm themselves), producing a sub-optimal arms race.
- **Q3:** What is Graham Allison's famous dictum in his Bureaucratic Politics Model?
  **A3:** "Where you sit determines where you stand" — meaning a government official's foreign policy stance is determined by the bureaucratic and budgetary interests of the department they lead.
