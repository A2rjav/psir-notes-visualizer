### Nature and Scope of International Relations
- **Archival Reference:** `page_057.png` to `page_059.png` (Anarchy & The Self-Help System).
- **The Defining Condition of International Politics:** Unlike domestic society, which is governed by a centralized government with a monopoly on legitimate violence, international relations exists in a permanent condition of **Anarchy** (the absence of a central world government).
- **The Self-Help Imperative:** Under systemic anarchy, no sovereign state can reliably entrust its survival to international law, treaties, or moral goodwill. The international system is a **"Self-Help" system** where each state must ultimately rely on its own national power, military preparedness, and strategic alliances to survive.
- **Three Core Paradigms of IR Theory:**
  1. *Realism:* Emphasizes power, national interest, conflict, and the enduring nature of state survival.
  2. *Liberalism:* Emphasizes international institutions, economic interdependence, democratic norms, and cooperation.
  3. *Marxism / Critical Theories:* Emphasizes global capitalist exploitation, dependency, class struggle, and ideological hegemony.

### E.H. Carr and the Critique of Inter-War Utopianism
- **Archival Reference:** `page_059.png` to `page_061.png` (The Twenty Years' Crisis, 1939).
- **The First Great Debate in IR (1930s):** Utopianism (Liberal Idealism) versus Realism.
- **E.H. Carr's Devastating Critique of Inter-War Utopianism:**
  - Following WWI, Western liberal statesmen (Woodrow Wilson) believed that international peace could be secured through the League of Nations, collective security, disarmament treaties, and international law.
  - Carr demonstrated that Utopianism suffered from a fatal delusion: assuming an innate **"Harmony of Interests"** between nations.
  - In reality, the so-called "international morality" preached by dominant powers (Britain, France, USA) was merely an ideological cloak to preserve their privileged territorial and economic status quo (*satisfied powers*) against rising, dissatisfied revisionist states (Germany, Japan, Italy).
  - Carr established that **Power is an irreducible, primary reality** of international politics that cannot be wished away by legal charters.

### Hans Morgenthau's Classical Realism (*Politics Among Nations*, 1948)
- **Archival Reference:** `page_061.png` to `page_064.png` (Morgenthau's Foundational Realist Canon).
- **Hans J. Morgenthau (1904–1980):** German-American political theorist; author of *Politics Among Nations: The Struggle for Power and Peace* (1948) — the foundational textbook that dominated post-WWII American strategic thought.
- **Rooted in Human Nature (Anthropological Realism):** Unlike modern structural realists who focus on systemic polarity, Morgenthau anchors realism in the flaws of **Human Nature**:
  - Human beings possess an insatiable, innate lust for power (*Animus Dominandi* — the desire to dominate others).
  - Because states are led by human beings, state behavior inevitably mirrors this lust for power.
  - *"International politics, like all politics, is a struggle for power. Whatever the ultimate aims of international politics, power is always the immediate aim."*

### Morgenthau's Six Principles of Political Realism
- **Archival Reference:** `page_064.png` to `page_068.png` (The Six Principles Detailed).
- **Principle 1: Objective Laws Rooted in Human Nature:**
  - Political realism believes that politics is governed by objective laws that have their roots in human nature.
  - Human nature has not changed since classical antiquity (Thucydides, Machiavelli); therefore, it is possible to develop a rational, timeless theory of international politics based on observable human behavior.
- **Principle 2: Concept of Interest Defined in Terms of Power:**
  - The main signpost that helps political realism find its way through the landscape of international politics is the concept of **"Interest Defined in Terms of Power"**.
  - This concept guards against two dangerous fallacies: the emotional fallacy of judging foreign policy by good moral intentions, and the intellectual fallacy of confusing foreign policy with personal ideology.
- **Principle 3: Interest is Dynamic, Contextual, but Power is Eternal:**
  - While the idea of interest is the essence of politics and is unaffected by the circumstances of time and place, the specific **content of national interest depends on the political and cultural context** within which foreign policy is formulated.
- **Principle 4: Tension between Moral Command and Political Prudence:**
  - Realism is not blind to morality, but insists that universal moral principles cannot be applied directly to the actions of states in their abstract formulation.
  - They must be filtered through the concrete circumstances of time and place.
  - **Prudence** — the weighing of the political consequences of alternative actions — is the supreme virtue in international politics.
- **Principle 5: Refusal to Identify Moral Aspirations of a Nation with Universal Laws:**
  - Political realism refuses to equate the moral aspirations of a particular nation (e.g., US crusade for democracy or Soviet crusade for communism) with the moral laws that govern the universe. All nations are tempted to clothe their selfish national ambitions in the language of universal virtue.
- **Principle 6: Autonomy of the Political Sphere:**
  - Realism maintains the intellectual autonomy of the political realm.
  - The political realist asks: "How does this policy affect the power of the nation?" — just as an economist asks: "How does this policy affect wealth?"
  - Subordinating foreign policy to legalism or moralism inevitably produces strategic disaster.

### Active Recall Flashcards (Batch 6)
- **Q1:** What was E.H. Carr's primary critique of inter-war liberal utopianism in *The Twenty Years' Crisis*?
  **A1:** Carr argued that utopianism falsely assumed a natural harmony of interests among nations, demonstrating that universal moral appeals were merely ideological tools used by dominant status-quo powers to preserve their privilege.
- **Q2:** How does Hans Morgenthau define the central signpost of Political Realism?
  **A2:** The concept of "National Interest Defined in Terms of Power," which allows analysts to evaluate foreign policy objectively, independent of rulers' subjective moral intentions or ideological rhetoric.
- **Q3:** What does Morgenthau consider the supreme virtue in international statecraft?
  **A3:** Prudence — the careful calculation of the concrete political and strategic consequences of alternative state actions, rather than the reckless pursuit of abstract moral ideals.
