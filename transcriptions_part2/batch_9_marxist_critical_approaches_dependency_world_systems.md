### Marxist Foundations: Lenin's Theory of Imperialism
- **Archival Reference:** `page_092.png` to `page_094.png` (Lenin's Imperialism, 1916).
- **V.I. Lenin's *Imperialism, the Highest Stage of Capitalism* (1916):**
  - Extended Marx's domestic critique of capitalism into a structural theory of international relations.
  - *Why Did Capitalism Not Collapse as Marx Predicted?* Because advanced capitalist economies solved their domestic crises of overproduction and falling rates of profit through **Imperialist Expansion**.
- **Five Key Characteristics of Imperialism:**
  1. Concentration of production into massive monopolies and cartels.
  2. Merging of bank capital with industrial capital to create **Finance Capital** and a financial oligarchy.
  3. The **Export of Capital** (surplus funds invested abroad for hyper-profits) replaces the mere export of merchandise goods.
  4. Formation of international monopolist capitalist cartels dividing global markets.
  5. The **Territorial Partition of the World** among major imperialist powers.
- **Inter-Imperialist Warfare:** Because the globe is finite, once the world is fully colonized, imperialist powers can only expand by re-dividing the world through catastrophic wars (Lenin's materialist explanation of World War I).

### Dependency Theory: Andre Gunder Frank
- **Archival Reference:** `page_094.png` to `page_097.png` (The Development of Underdevelopment, 1966).
- **Rejection of Modernization Theory:** Western modernization theorists (W.W. Rostow's *Five Stages of Growth*) claimed that underdeveloped societies are merely at an early traditional stage, and will naturally develop by adopting Western capital, technology, and democratic institutions.
- **Andre Gunder Frank's Revolutionary Thesis:** Underdevelopment is **not an original traditional state**; it is the direct structural historical product of centuries of capitalist exploitation: **"The Development of Underdevelopment"**.
- **The Metropolis-Satellite Model:**
  - The global capitalist system is structured as an exploitative hierarchy of **Metropolises (Core)** and **Satellites (Periphery)**.
  - Wealth, raw materials, and economic surplus are extracted from rural satellites, funneled to peripheral national capitals, and systematically expropriated by the metropolitan centers in North America and Western Europe.
- **The Comprador Bourgeoisie:** Post-colonial elites (bureaucrats, politicians, Westernized merchants) act as client agents (**Compradors**) of foreign capital, enriching themselves while keeping their domestic populations trapped in poverty.
- **Prescription: De-linking:** Peripheral nations can never achieve genuine autonomous development within the capitalist world system; they must break their exploitative links through socialist revolution and self-reliance.

### Immanuel Wallerstein's World Systems Theory
- **Archival Reference:** `page_097.png` to `page_100.png` (The Modern World-System, 1974).
- **Unit of Analysis:** Rejects the state-centric approach. The only meaningful unit of social and economic analysis is the **World System** — a unified spatial entity with a single division of labor and multiple cultural systems.
- **The Modern Capitalist World-Economy:** Emerged in 16th-century Europe, expanding to encompass the entire globe. Driven by the relentless accumulation of capital.
- **Tripartite Structural Hierarchy:**
  1. *The Core (North America, Western Europe, Japan):* High-profit, high-technology, capital-intensive production; diversified economies; strong, bureaucratically centralized states; high wages and skilled labour.
  2. *The Periphery (Sub-Saharan Africa, parts of Latin America & Asia):* Low-profit, low-technology, labor-intensive extraction of raw materials, minerals, and cash crops; weak, corrupt state apparatuses; coerced, low-wage labour; vulnerable to unequal exchange.
  3. *The Semi-Periphery (India, China, Brazil, South Africa, Mexico):*
     - Wallerstein's most brilliant theoretical innovation.
     - Represents an intermediate structural zone exhibiting characteristics of both Core and Periphery.
     - Exploits the Periphery while being exploited by the Core.
     - *Political Function:* Acts as a **structural shock absorber** preventing global systemic polarization and revolution, just as the middle class stabilizes domestic capitalist societies.

### Robert Cox and Critical Theory: Theory for Whom and for What?
- **Archival Reference:** `page_100.png` to `page_101.png` (Social Forces, States and World Orders, 1981).
- **Robert Cox's Famous Epistemological Dictum:**
  - *"Theory is always for someone and for some purpose."*
  - There is no such thing as a neutral, objective, value-free theory of international relations. All theories reflect specific class interests, historical epochs, and political agendas.
- **Two Major Types of Theory:**
  1. *Problem-Solving Theory (Realism, Neo-Liberalism):* Takes the existing world order, power distributions, and institutions as given and fixed, seeking to make them function smoothly and manage crises for the ruling status-quo elite.
  2. *Critical Theory:* Questions the historical origins of existing institutions, exposes hidden power structures and class exploitation, and explores possibilities for radical human emancipation.

### Feminist Approaches to IR: J. Ann Tickner's Critique of Morgenthau
- **Archival Reference:** `page_101.png` to `page_102.png` (Gender in International Relations, 1992).
- **Deconstructing the Masculinist Canon:** J. Ann Tickner demonstrated that classical realism is not an objective universal science, but a **deeply gendered, masculinist partial perspective**.
- **Tickner's Feminist Reformulation of Morgenthau's Six Principles:**
  1. Objectivity is socially constructed; human nature is multidimensional, incorporating both masculine assertiveness and feminine nurturance and connection.
  2. National interest cannot be defined solely in terms of power; true national security requires cooperation, ecological sustainability, and human security.
  3. Power cannot be understood exclusively as domination and control over others; power is also **empowerment and capacity to act collectively** (drawing on Hannah Arendt).
  4. Rejecting the separation of private morality from public politics; political actions cannot be excused from ethical responsibility for human suffering.
  5. The pursuit of common moral ground among diverse cultures is possible through dialogical empathy rather than imperialist universalism.
  6. The political sphere is inseparable from the social and economic spheres; divorcing politics from human reproduction, caregiving, and social welfare marginalizes women's lives.

### Active Recall Flashcards (Batch 9)
- **Q1:** What was V.I. Lenin's materialist explanation for why capitalism avoided domestic collapse in *Imperialism*?
  **A1:** Capitalism temporarily solved crises of overproduction and falling profits by exporting surplus capital to colonize and exploit the underdeveloped world, leading inevitably to inter-imperialist wars.
- **Q2:** What are the three structural tiers of the global economy in Immanuel Wallerstein's World Systems Theory?
  **A2:** The Core (high-tech, capital-intensive, strong states), the Periphery (raw material extraction, low-wage labor, weak states), and the Semi-Periphery (intermediate structural shock absorber).
- **Q3:** What is the significance of Robert Cox's dictum: "Theory is always for someone and for some purpose"?
  **A3:** It asserts that no IR theory is politically neutral or objective; theories like Realism function as "problem-solving" frameworks that legitimize and preserve existing hegemonic power structures.
