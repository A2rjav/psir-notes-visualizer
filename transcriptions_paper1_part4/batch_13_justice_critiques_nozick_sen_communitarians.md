According to communitarians, it is not possible to overlook community. Community is natural organic. It is bigger than Individual. Individuals good is not independent of the common good.

#### (iii) Concept of Justice:
According to Liberals there can be a universal principle of justice. Justice is a product of reason in man. Liberal concept of Justice is based on the concept of Atomistic man. If Liberals take universalistic view of justice, communitarians take particularistic view of justice. The sense of justice will vary from society to society. The idea of justice cannot be independent of time space & culture.

#### (iv) Concept of State:
According to Liberals state should adopt value neutrality. State should be indifferent towards different ways of life. There should be seperation between personal & political. Liberals propose universal citizenship. Liberals relive [rely] on the idea of Nationalism to achieve solidarity.

According to communitarians, state need not be neutral or indifferent. State should promote such values which strengthens social solidarity. State should not expect that person will become so rational to forget his communities, customs & traditions. State should adopt cultural relativist perspective. State should not attempt to achieve homogenisation for social solidarity. Justice is not universalising. Justice is an Art of differentiation. According to them, there is no need to keep religion out of public sphere because that is not possible. It is better if we recognise it & give space in public policies. Communitarians supports consociational democracy rather than Majoritarian democracy.

#### • Sandel's Theory of Justice :-
**Book** — *'Liberalism & Limits of Justice'* [1982]

Sandel has criticised rawl's theory of justice on two major grounds:
1. Rawls conception of self.
2. Rawls conception of society.

##### (i) Rawls conception of self:
According to Sandel, man is embedded self. Man is never in original position. Original position is an abstract idea. People are not abstract individuals making choices, they are moral agents (members of society) located in time & space, embedded in network of relations. For Rawls self is prior to end. But according to Sandel self is constituted by end. People take decisions in real life on the basis of what they consider as right. However, it is never a free choice. Hence, the concept of justice can be understood only within the framework of community & not independent of it.

##### (ii) Rawls conception of society:
Rawls should have taken much fuller view of community. Concept of Rights cannot be detached from communities culture & tradition. It is not possible to adopt value neutrality. It is not understandable as to why we should not affirm our identities in public sphere.

#### • Michael Walzer
**Book** — *The Spheres of Justice* [1983]

> "In the words of Walzer, 'Different goods ought to be distributed differently. According to the different mean-ing attached to them by different groups, in accordance to the different procedures by different agents & all the differences are derived from the different understanding which are cinevitable [inevitable] products of differences in history & culture.'"

**What is Reasonable pluralism?**  
In liberal countries, persons follows various comprehensive doctrines.  
*Example of comprehensive doctrine:* Liberalism — as philosophy of life, Christianity, Islam, Judaism.

**Reason of stability in these societies?**  
According to Rawls, the reason is democratic political culture.

**What are the features of Democratic Political culture?**  
1. Value of equal citizenship.
2. People are willing to offer fair terms to each other.
3. People stay together for right reason rather than compulsion or bargaining.

— Rawls uses the term **overlapping consensus**.

According to Rawls, overlapping consensus is not **Modus Vivendi**.

**What is Modus Vivendi?**  
Modus Vivendi show bargain guided by self interest. Overlapping consensus is based on moral convection [conviction] of fairness.

**What is overlapping consensus?**  
Reasonable & Moral people living in democratic political culture are able to develop overlapping consensus on 'Political conception of Justice' for particular reasons.

**What is Political conception of Justice?**  
Political conception of Justice is based on the principle of Justice as fairness with some literal modification.  
According to Rawls, Political conception of Justice is **'free standing idea'**.

### Page 194: Clamshell Auction, Thin Veil, Insurance, Envy Test & Luck Egalitarianism

```
Join Telegram for More Update : - https://t.me/upsc_pdf
```

In Rawls theory people are behind **Thick veil of Ignorance** but in Dworkin they are behind **thin veil of Ignorance**. They know their talents, advantages, disadvantages, but do not know the actual value of their talents. It can be decided only by Auction. Value cannot be decided by the person. Value depends on how much market is willing to pay. In the end of the Auction it may happen that persons talent has the least demand in the market. Hence, people will go for keeping some clamshell as a Insurance. Hence a common pool can be created. 

Dworkin proposes **Envy Test** that distribution is fair which does not result into the feeling of Envy. When a person is satisfied with his own bundle then feeling jealous for the bundle of the others [no one prefers anyone else's bundle of goods].

Dworkin gives the concept of **option luck** as well as **Brute luck**. Insurance can compensate both Auction and Brute luck. Dworkin suggest that common pool can be used for the benefit of those who suffer from Brute luck. Suppose a person suffers from some physical disability he will have to keep some extra clamshell to compensate for the disadvantages. Hence he cannot enter into the Auction with the same level of equality. Such person should be given extra clamshell to compensate for Brute luck. 

According to Dworkin Libertarian arguement that people are responsible & should be held responsible for the outcomes of their choices does not apply. According to Dworkin people are not responsible, should not be held responsible for the unchoosen circumstances in which they make choices. Hence he suggest **Endowment sensitive auction** ie. giving extra clamshells to compensate disadvantages.

```
Join Telegram for More Update : - https://t.me/upsc_pdf

##### ③ Amartya Sen's Theory of Equality:
He suggest to make people equal not just in resources but in **capabilities**. Amartya Sen gives two concepts:

1. **Capability:** Capability is the ability to do what one wants. Capability is absence of deprivation.
2. **Functionings:** Functionings is 'being & doing'. Functioning is doing what one wants to do, it is a source of well being.

*Eg:* If we give two children the bicycles one knows cycling and the other does not know, they cannot have equal level of functioning.

He compares **hunger & fasting**. Hunger represent deprivation as hunger cannot be choice whereas fasting can be a choice & itself a source of functioning.

Hence he suggest state to adopt capability approach. Poverty is not just the absence of income it is presence of deprivation. Resource equality is equality of means whereas equality of capability is a equality of ends. The ends being functioning.

Capability approach is better because it takes into account the diversity. Different people may have different types of deprivation.  
*Eg:* In Indian society the nature of deprivation which a poor girl child may suffer are going to be the different from the deprivation which poor male child will suffer.

Capability approach focus on Human developement. In Capability approach people are not means of economic growth rather ends of economic growth. Thus he defines developement as freedom.

### A. Libertarian Critique: Robert Nozick (*Anarchy, State, and Utopia*, 1974)

Robert Nozick provides the foundational libertarian / neo-liberal critique of John Rawls's redistributive welfare state. Nozick grounds his theory in Immanuel Kant's categorical imperative (second formulation): **"Act in such a way that you treat humanity, whether in your own person or in the person of any other, never merely as a means to an end, but always at the same time as an end."**

           TAXATION = FORCED LABOUR       STATE WELFARE
           (Appropriating hours of       (Treats Talented as
            life without consent)         mere Means for Others)
```

#### 1. The Entitlement Theory of Justice (Three Principles)
Nozick argues that justice is purely historical and unpatterned, contrasting sharply with Rawls's patterned and end-state difference principle:
1. **Principle of Just Initial Acquisition**: Grounded in John Locke's labour theory of property, subject to the **Lockean Proviso** ("enough and as good left in common for others"). An individual acquires an unowned resource by mixing their labour with it, provided others' condition is not worsened net of compensation.
2. **Principle of Just Transfer**: Voluntary exchange, gift, trade, or contract without fraud, theft, or coercion. If a holding was justly acquired, its voluntary transfer yields a just entitlement.
3. **Principle of Rectification of Injustice**: Historical violations of principles (1) and (2) (e.g., colonial conquest, enslavement, expropriation) must be identified and rectified through compensatory transfers. However, as noted in Shubhra Ranjan's lecture (Page 187), Nozick insists that **historical injustice cannot be stretched too far into present times**, because tracing counterfactual ownership chains across generations becomes epistemically impossible and politically dangerous.

#### 2. The Wilt Chamberlain Argument (Liberty Upsets Patterns)
To dismantle Rawls's "patterned" distribution (e.g., Difference Principle D1), Nozick presents the famous Wilt Chamberlain thought experiment:
- Suppose society starts in distribution $D_1$ (e.g., Rawlsian equality or difference principle distribution).
- Wilt Chamberlain is a basketball star in high demand. Chamberlain signs a contract: for every home game, 25 cents from each ticket price goes directly to him.
- 1 million fans voluntarily drop an extra 25 cents into a separate box to watch Chamberlain play.
- At the end of the season, Chamberlain has earned \$250,000, creating distribution $D_2$.
- **Nozick's Core Dilemma**: $D_2$ is no longer patterned ($D_1$ is broken). Yet, every step from $D_1$ to $D_2$ was completely voluntary! To maintain a pattern like Rawls's difference principle, the state must:
  > *"Continuously interfere in people's lives to stop them from transferring resources as they wish, or continuously confiscate from individuals resources that others chose to give them."*  
  **Conclusion**: *"Liberty upsets patterns."*

#### 3. "Taxation is Forced Labour"
Nozick equates income taxation for redistribution to slavery:
- If the state takes 25% of your income earned through 40 hours of weekly labour, it is effectively forcing you to work 10 hours a week unpaid for the state's chosen beneficiaries.
- It violates **self-ownership**. Taking the earnings of $n$ hours of labour is identical to taking $n$ hours from the person; it forces the person to work for another's purpose.

#### 4. The Minimal / Nightwatchman State & The "Single Well" Exception
- Nozick rejects the welfare state and advocates the **Ultra-Minimal State** expanding strictly into the **Minimal State** (protecting against force, theft, fraud, and enforcement of contracts).
- **The "Single Well" Exception (Lockean Proviso in Extremis)**: As highlighted on Page 187 of the lecture notes:
  - If a village has a single well and the water owner arbitrarily refuses water to dying villagers, the state has the legitimate authority to intervene, use force, and make water available.
  - This is because the owner's monopolization violates the Lockean Proviso by drastically worsening the baseline survival of others, endangering life itself.

### B. Communitarian Critique: Sandel, Walzer, MacIntyre, Taylor

Communitarians emerged in the 1980s as the primary philosophical counterweight to Rawlsian deontological liberalism.

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                       LIBERALISM vs COMMUNITARIANISM                        │
├───────────────────────────────┬─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ Dimension                     │ Communitarian Critique                      │
├───────────────────────────────┼─────────────────────────────────────────────┤
│ 1. Conception of Self         │ Embedded / Situated Self (Sandel, MacIntyre)│
│ 2. Universal vs Particular    │ Particularistic / Cultural Meanings (Walzer)│
│ 3. State Neutrality           │ Politics of Common Good / Consociationalism │
│ 4. Nature of Justice          │ "Art of Differentiation" (Walzer)           │
│ 5. Priority of Right vs Good  │ Priority of the Good over the Right (Taylor)│
└───────────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

#### 1. Michael Sandel (*Liberalism and the Limits of Justice*, 1982)
- **Critique of the "Unencumbered Self"**: Rawls assumes an autonomous agent who can step behind the Veil of Ignorance, detached from all social bonds, religious traditions, and communal ties. Sandel argues this "unencumbered self" is an ontological illusion.
- **The "Embedded Self" (Situated Self)**: Human beings are born into families, cultures, and traditions that constitute their moral identities. The self does not precede its ends; **the self is constituted by its ends**.
- **Self is Prior to End vs. End Constituting the Self**: For Rawls, *"the self is prior to the ends which are affirmed by it"*. For Sandel, moral deliberation is not an abstract choice of ends, but a process of discovery of who we already are within our communities.
- **Critique of State Neutrality**: Sandel rejects liberal value-neutrality. If justice ignores community, citizens cannot affirm their deepest moral and religious convictions in public discourse, leading to civic disaffection and the rise of intolerant fundamentalisms.

#### 2. Michael Walzer (*Spheres of Justice: A Defense of Pluralism and Equality*, 1983)
- **Rejection of "Simple Equality"**: Simple equality attempts to distribute all social goods (money, health, education, political power) according to a single master formula. This leads to tyranny.
- **Complex Equality**: No social good $X$ should be distributed to men and women who possess another good $Y$ merely because they possess $Y$ without regard to the meaning of $X$.
  - Example: Wealth should not buy political office; political power should not buy medical care.
- **Spheres of Justice & Social Meanings**: Goods have specific social meanings created by distinct communities across history:
  > *"Different goods ought to be distributed differently, according to the different meaning attached to them by different groups, in accordance to the different procedures by different agents."*
- **Empirical Illustrations from Lecture Notes (Page 183)**:
  - *Indian Caste System*: Persists because historically, people internalised and subscribed to caste-based occupational and social meanings.
  - *Ancient Greece*: Citizens preferred spending collective wealth on public gymnasiums and public theatres rather than direct welfare transfers to the poor, because Athenian civic glory was their shared conception of the good.

#### 3. Alasdair MacIntyre (*After Virtue*, 1981)
- Modern liberal society is in moral crisis because it attempts to construct universal justice out of deracinated, atomistic individuals.
- MacIntyre argues that moral agency is narrative in character: *"I can only answer the question 'What am I to do?' if I can answer the prior question 'Of what story or stories do I find myself a part?'"*

#### 4. Charles Taylor (*Sources of the Self*, 1989 & *Atomism*, 1985)
- Taylor attacks liberal **"Atomism"**—the notion that an individual possesses rights independent of society.
- Freedom is not an individual endowment; it is an achievement of an advanced civilization. True autonomy is only realizable within a rich communal and linguistic framework.

#### 1. Gerald A. Cohen's Egalitarian Critique of Incentives
- Cohen takes aim at Rawls's justification of inequality under the Difference Principle (inequalities are just if they motivate talented people to work harder, generating wealth that benefits the worst-off).
- Cohen calls this the **"Kidnapper's Ransom Argument"**: The talented are demanding economic incentives (tax cuts, bonuses) to perform work they are naturally equipped to do. If the talented were genuinely committed to Rawls's difference principle, they would not demand higher compensation as an extortionate condition for working harder!
- True justice requires not merely just basic institutional structures (*basic structure of society*), but an **Egalitarian Ethos** that informs personal choices in everyday life: *"The personal is political."*

#### 2. Susan Moller Okin's Feminist Critique
- As noted in Lecture 46 (Page 188), Susan Moller Okin demonstrates that Rawls assumes the heads of households behind the Veil of Ignorance are traditional male patriarchs.
- **The Family as the First School of Justice**: If justice is the first virtue of social institutions, the family must be just. Yet historically, the traditional gender-structured family is rooted in unequal divisions of unpaid domestic care work, economic dependence, and vulnerability to domestic abuse.
- Until justice penetrates the private sphere of the home, women cannot achieve genuine equal citizenship or fair equality of opportunity in the public sphere.

### D. Realization-Focused Critique: Amartya Sen (*The Idea of Justice*, 2009)

Amartya Sen synthesises classical Indian jurisprudence with modern social choice theory to challenge Rawlsian "Transcendental Institutionalism".

```
                          AMARTYA SEN'S FRAMEWORK
                                     │
           ┌─────────────────────────┴─────────────────────────┐
           ▼                                                   ▼
     NITI (Procedural)                                NYAYA (Realization)
  - Institutional correctness                      - Actual lived outcomes
  - Transcendental blueprints                      - Elimination of manifest
  - Rational choice (abstract)                       injustices (Buddha-inspired)
  - Focus on universal rules                       - Social choice (real persons)
```

#### 1. Niti vs. Nyaya
- **Niti**: Institutional perfection, organizational correctness, behavioral rules, and legal procedures (Rawls's primary focus).
- **Nyaya**: What actually emerges in the real lives of people; realization-focused outcomes, avoiding manifest horrors and starvation.
- Sen argues that focusing exclusively on *Niti* produces institutional blindness, whereas *Nyaya* demands confronting actual human misery immediately (inspired by the **Buddha's** quest to eliminate human suffering).

#### 2. Transcendental Institutionalism vs. Comparative Realization
- **Transcendental Institutionalism** (Rawls, Kant): Seeks to define what a "perfectly just society" would look like in an ideal world.
- **Comparative Realization** (Sen, Adam Smith, Condorcet, Mary Wollstonecraft): Compares available policy options to make society *less unjust*. We do not need an abstract agreement on what the "ideal picture" of a Mona Lisa looks like to recognize that a painting has been severely damaged and needs restoration.

#### 3. The Parable of the Three Children and the Flute
Sen demonstrates that rational, moral individuals can arrive at completely incompatible, yet equally valid, conceptions of justice:
- **Anne** claims the flute because she is the *only one who can play it* (Utilitarian / Functioning-based justice).
- **Bob** claims the flute because he is *so poor he has no other toys* (Economic Egalitarian / Difference Principle / Marxist need-based justice).
- **Carla** claims the flute because *she made it with her own labour* (Libertarian / Lockean / Nozickian entitlement theory).
- **The Takeaway**: There is no universal, impartial procedure (like the Original Position) that can resolve these three conflicting claims without arbitrary prioritization.

### E. Luck Egalitarianism: Ronald Dworkin (*Sovereign Virtue*, 2000)

Ronald Dworkin establishes "Equality of Resources", positioning Equality as the **"Sovereign Virtue"** of political morality.

```
                         DWORKIN'S LUCK EGALITARIANISM
                                       │
                      ┌────────────────┴────────────────┐
                      ▼                                 ▼
                 OPTION LUCK                        BRUTE LUCK
             - Calculated gambles               - Unchosen natural lotteries
             - Voluntary risks                  - Congenital disability, genetic
             - Individual is RESPONSIBLE          defects, sudden accidents
             - NO state compensation            - STATE MUST COMPENSATE
                                                  (via underwritten insurance pool)
```

#### 1. Critique of "Equality of Welfare" (Pages 192–193)
- Utilitarians advocate equalising pleasure or subjective satisfaction.
- **The Expensive Taste Trap (The BMW vs. Alto Parable)**:
  - If Son A develops an expensive taste (needs a BMW to feel happy) while Son B is content with an Alto, an equality-of-welfare state must transfer massively more money to Son A to make them equally happy.
  - As Dworkin famously writes (Page 193):
    > *"Equality of Welfare would require all of us to keep compensating someone who messes up with his life over & over, to keep his level of welfare as everyone elses, but no sensible egalitarianism would agree."*

#### 2. The Desert Island Shipwreck & Clamshell Auction (Page 193)
- Shipwrecked survivors land on an uninhabited island with abundant natural resources.
- Everyone receives **equal purchasing power**: 100 identical clamshells.
- An **Ambition-Sensitive Auction** is held where individuals bid for resources based on their personal life ambitions.
- **The Envy Test**: The auction succeeds and is just if and only if at the end, **no person prefers any other person's bundle of resources over their own**.

#### 3. Thin Veil of Ignorance & Endowment Sensitivity (Page 194)
- **Rawls's Thick Veil vs. Dworkin's Thin Veil**:
  - Rawls: People know nothing of their natural talents, strengths, or weaknesses.
  - Dworkin: People know their physical talents, traits, and skills, but do not know the market value of those talents in the island economy.
- **Hypothetical Insurance Market**:
  - What if someone suffers from physical disabilities or unmarketable skills (**Brute Luck**)?
  - Behind the thin veil, individuals will rationally pool some clamshells to buy insurance against congenital disabilities or talent deficits.
  - Thus, Dworkin designs an **Endowment-Sensitive Auction**: individuals suffering from unchosen disadvantages are given compensatory extra clamshells from the collective insurance pool.
- **Core Ethos**: Society must be **ambition-sensitive** (respecting choices) but **endowment-insensitive** (nullifying natural handicaps).

### Matrix 2: Theories of Equality (Welfare vs. Resources vs. Capability)

| Dimension | Equality of Welfare (Utilitarians) | Equality of Resources (Ronald Dworkin) | Equality of Capabilities (Amartya Sen) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Philosophical Goal** | Equalising subjective pleasure, satisfaction, or utility | Equalising external means/endowments and insurable inputs | Equalising effective substantive freedoms to achieve valued functionings |
| **Primary Metric** | Mental state / Preference satisfaction | Resources (Clamshells, Bundles of goods) | Capabilities (combinations of functionings: 'beings & doings') |
| **Handling of Choices** | Penalised: forces society to fund expensive tastes | **Ambition-Sensitive**: individuals pay full cost of chosen tastes | Individuals are autonomous agents choosing from a capability set |
| **Handling of Handicaps** | Compensated only if handicap produces subjective misery | **Endowment-Sensitive**: compensated through hypothetical insurance | Compensated directly: focuses on conversion factors (e.g. bicycle example) |
| **Means vs. Ends** | Collapses means into subjective ends | Focuses purely on means (resources) | **Resources are Means; Capabilities & Functionings are Ends** |
| **Poverty Definition** | Low utility / unhappiness | Asset / resource deficit | **Deprivation of basic capabilities** (not merely low income) |
