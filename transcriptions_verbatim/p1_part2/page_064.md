Q. What is co-habitation? Give illustrations & the
consequences?
Co-habitation is a term used in french political system
In franch there is a semi-presidential form of Govt.
Co-habitation is a situation where President belongs to
different party & PM belongs to different party. In
such situation the relationship b/w President & PM is
not as smooth as it should be. In case of India also
there have been occassions of co-habitation. Co-habi-
tation has created the situation of Presidential Acti-
vism. According to James Manor, many Presidents in
India have been Activist Presidents. If we take the
example of Dr. Rajendra Prasad despite belonging
to same party he was activist president. Gyani Jail
singh was also criticised when he used pocket veto
over the bill proposed by Rajiv Gandhi Govt. w.r.t.
amendment in post office.
President K. R. Narayanan was one of the most activist
President. Eg.
(i) He was the 1st one to reject the ~~imposition~~ recommendation
for imposition of Art. 356 in context of state of UP.
(ii) Against the tradition of President reading the speech
provided by the ministry on the eve of Republic Day
he preferred to give interview on Doordarshan.
(iii) He insisted to read his own speech rather than provided
by MEA on the occassion of the visit of US President
Bill Clinton.
