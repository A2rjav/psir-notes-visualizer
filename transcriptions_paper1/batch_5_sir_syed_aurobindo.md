### 1. SIR SYED AHMED KHAN: CRITIQUE, EVOLUTION & RELEVANCE

#### A. Dual Front of Criticism
1. **Orthodox Muslim Section (Ulema & Traditional Clergy):**
   - Sir Syed was denounced as a heretic (*Kafir*, *Nechari* / Naturalist) because of his rationalist reinterpretation of the Quran in light of modern Western science.
   - His rejection of blind imitation (*Taqlid*) and advocacy of independent reasoning (*Ijtihad*) enraged traditional Islamic scholars (such as the Deoband school founded by Muhammad Qasim Nanautawi and Rashid Ahmad Gangohi in 1866).
2. **Indian Nationalists / Indian National Congress (INC):**
   - Sir Syed came under heavy nationalist criticism for advising Muslims to strictly stay away from the INC (founded 1885).
   - He believed Indian Muslims were educationally, economically, and administratively backward compared to Hindus. Joining competitive democratic politics under majority rule would reduce Muslims to permanent political subjection.

#### B. The Transition: From Composite Nationalism to Two-Nation Genesis
* **Initial Phase (Composite Indian Identity):**
  - Famous metaphor: Hindus and Muslims are the "two beautiful eyes of a lovely bride (India)." If one is injured, the beauty of the bride is impaired.
  - Advised Muslims against cow slaughter/beef eating to respect Hindu neighbourly sensibilities.
* **The Turning Point: Awadh / United Provinces Language Controversy (1867):**
  - Movement by Hindu leaders to replace Urdu (written in Persian-Arabic script) with Hindi (written in Devanagari script) as the official court language of the North-Western Provinces and Awadh.
  - Sir Syed concluded: Language is inextricably tied to cultural identity and economic livelihood (government jobs). If Hindus and Muslims could not even agree on a common official medium, political harmony was impossible.
  - He prophesied to British official Mr. Shakespeare: *"Now I am convinced that these two communities will not join wholeheartedly in anything... Hostility between the two will increase immensely."*
* **Originator of Two-Nation Theory:**
  - Posited that Hindus and Muslims constitute two distinct, mutually irreducible historical, cultural, and political entities with divergent interests that would irreconcilably clash under Western representative democracy based on simple majoritarian headcount.

#### C. Reconciliation with the British Raj
* ***Asbab-e-Baghawat-e-Hind* (1858, *Causes of the Indian Revolt*):**
  - Addressed directly to the British parliament and administration.
  - Demonstrated that the 1857 Revolt was not an exclusively Muslim anti-British conspiracy, but the direct consequence of bureaucratic arrogance, lack of Indian representation in legislative bodies, and insensitive administrative policies.
* ***The Loyal Muhammadans of India* (1860–1861):**
  - Periodical accounts detailing instances where Muslims remained steadfastly loyal to the British during the 1857 crisis.
  - Objective: Dispel British suspicion and end the post-1857 targeted political and economic persecution of the Muslim community.
* **Institutional Achievement:**
  - British patronage secured; establishment of the **Muhammadan Anglo-Oriental (MAO) College** at Aligarh in 1875 (modelled after Oxford and Cambridge), which blossomed into **Aligarh Muslim University (AMU)** in 1920.

#### D. Contemporary Evaluation & Relevance
* Though historically contested due to the separatist trajectory of the Aligarh Movement that culminated in the 1947 Partition, Sir Syed represents the archetype of a **modernizing, pragmatic reformer**.
* Contemporary relevance: As highlighted by the **Sachar Committee Report (2006)** and the **Ranganath Misra Commission**, contemporary Indian Muslims continue to face severe educational backwardness, socio-economic marginalization, and psychological alienation. Sir Syed's message of modern scientific education, rational inquiry, pragmatic self-reliance, and educational empowerment remains indispensable.

### 2. SRI AUROBINDO GHOSH (1872–1950): THE PROPHET OF INDIAN NATIONALISM

#### A. Intellectual Context & Synthesis of East and West
* **Title:** Known as the **"Prophet of Indian Nationalism"** (a moniker championed by Dr. Karan Singh and Haridas & Uma Mukherjee).
* **Rabindranath Tagore's Tribute:** *"The world will get the message of India through Aurobindo Ghosh... I say to him: 'Aurobindo, accept the salutations of Rabindranath.'"*
* **The Grand Synthesis:**
  - Aurobindo was educated at St Paul's School, London, and King's College, Cambridge, gaining masterly command over Western classical and modern thought, before returning to India to deeply study Sanskrit, Vedanta, and the Upanishads.
  - **Critique of East and West:**
    - The **West** developed material science, individual liberty, and institutional vitality, but neglected the spiritual dimension, descending into mechanical materialism and aggressive imperialism.
    - The **East (India)** scaled the heights of spiritual realization, but neglected material development and physical organization, resulting in stagnation, poverty, and foreign subjugation.
    - **Synthesis:** East needs the West for material vitality; West needs the East for spiritual redemption.
* **India as *Spiritual Guide to Humanity* (*Jagadguru*):**
  - India's freedom is not an end in narrow chauvinism or political self-gratification.
  - India must be free so that she can fulfill her cosmic mission: to deliver the spiritual message of Sanatana Dharma to rescue global civilization from soul-destroying materialism.

#### B. Philosophical Roots: Neo-Vedantism & Bharat Mata
* **Swami Vivekananda's Neo-Vedanta:**
  - Aurobindo transformed Vivekananda's spiritual awakening ("Arise, awake, and stop not till the goal is achieved"; serving the *Daridra Narayana*) into political nationalism.
* **Bankim Chandra Chatterjee's *Anandamath*:**
  - Bankim conceptualized the Motherland not as a physical tract of land, but as the living embodiment of the Divine Mother (**Bharat Mata**), venerated through the hymn *Vande Mataram*.
  - Aurobindo elevated this literary imagery into an absolute political-theological doctrine.

### 4. THEORY OF NATIONALISM: DETAILED EXPOSITION

#### A. Colonial Challenge vs. Early Nationalist Response
| Dimension | Imperialist / Colonial View | Moderate Nationalist View | Aurobindo Ghosh's Radical View |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Ontology of India** | "India is merely a geographical expression" (Sir John Strachey, Sir John Seeley). No nation, just a mosaic of warring tribes/castes. | Accepts India is not a finished nation, but a *"Nation in the Making"* (Surendranath Banerjea). | **India was, is, and will always be an eternal, organic, spiritual nation.** |
| **View of British Rule** | Legitimate civilizing mission; *Pax Britannica*. | *"A blessing in disguise"*; source of English education, modern laws, railway, and science. | **An unmitigated evil and engine of economic and spiritual exploitation.** |
| **Methods** | Bureaucratic repression / concessions. | Mild constitutionalism: **Prayers, Petitions, Protests (3Ps)**; annual Congress Pandal sessions. | **Mendicancy (Beggary).** Demands direct action, passive resistance, complete non-cooperation, or armed revolution. |
| **Ultimate Goal** | Permanent imperial domination / eventual dominion status. | Self-government within the British Empire (Good Governance / *Suraj*). | **Complete Freedom (*Purna Swaraj*) / Liberation** as an unconditional spiritual right. |

#### B. Critique of the Moderates: *New Lamps for Old* (1893)
* In a devastating series of articles in the Bombay journal *Induprakash*, the 21-year-old Aurobindo dissected the Indian National Congress:
  - INC under the Moderates represented a timid, bourgeois leadership alienated from the Indian masses.
  - Mocked Moderate belief that a nation can be created out of rhetoric in annual winter "Congress Pandals".
  - Declared: *"Nationalism is not dependent on Congress sessions... The Congress was dying of consumption; it was playing with bubbles."*

#### C. The Metaphysical Definition: What Nation Is NOT vs. What Nation IS
Aurobindo's famous poetic-philosophical liturgy defining the nation:

* **Negative Propositions (What the Nation is NOT):**
  - *Not a mere geographical expression.*
  - *Not a piece of land.*
  - *Not a collection of human beings.*
  - *Not a figure of speech.*
  - *Not a creation of the mind.*
  - *Not the figment of people's imagination.*
  - *Not a political programme.*
  - *Not an intellectual pastime.*
* **Positive Axioms (What the Nation IS):**
  - **Nation is a FAITH.**
  - **Nation is a RELIGION.**
  - **Nation is a SPIRIT.**
  - **Nation is DIVINE (*Sanatana Dharma* / Living Shakti).**
* **The Inevitability of Victory:**
  - Because the Nation is the incarnation of the Divine Spirit, no imperialist power on earth can crush it.
  - *"Nationalism cannot be suppressed; it will emerge on its own because it is immortal."*

#### D. Swaraj vs. Suraj: Delinking Governance from Freedom
* **Bal Gangadhar Tilak:** Defined *Swaraj* pragmatically as *Suraj* (Good Governance / Home Rule by natives under British paramountcy).
* **Aurobindo Ghosh:**
  - Delinked *Swaraj* from *Suraj*.
  - Even if foreign rule could provide the best governance in the world, it remains intolerable because foreign subjugation destroys the national soul (*Svadharma*).
  - **Swaraj is not a means to an end; Swaraj is a supreme value and an end in itself.**

### 5. STRATEGY OF RESISTANCE: CRITIQUE OF MENDICANCY & DIRECT ACTION

#### A. Rejection of "Political Mendicancy"
* Constitutional methods assume a constitutional relationship. The British Government in India was an autocracy unaccountable to Indians; begging for reforms was futile and dishonourable.
* *"Instead of Prayers, we require Resistance — either resistance or revolution."*
* Maintained direct underground links with secret societies (e.g., *Anushilan Samiti*, *Yugantar* with Barindra Kumar Ghosh and Jatindranath Mukherjee/Bagha Jatin).

#### B. Striking at the Root: The Fourfold Boycott & Swadeshi
To force the colonial state to collapse, the national movement had to undermine the material and administrative benefits Britain extracted from India:

1. **Economic Boycott:** Refusal to purchase British manufactured goods; revival of indigenous cottage industries (*Swadeshi*).
2. **Administrative Boycott:** Complete refusal of Indians to serve in government administrative machinery, proving that Indians can run their own affairs without colonial masters.
3. **Judicial Boycott:** Boycott of British-run colonial courts and establishment of indigenous popular arbitration tribunals (*Panchayats* / Arbitration Sabhas).
4. **Educational Boycott & National Education:**
   - Denounced Macaulay's education system as an instrument designed to produce loyal, subservient clerks and suppress national consciousness (*"British schools kill nationalist sentiments"*).
   - Called for **National Education** (Aurobindo served as the first Principal of the Bengal National College, 1906) to inculcate national pride, mother-tongue instruction, and scientific-spiritual values.
