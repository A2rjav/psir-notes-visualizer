#### 3. Scholarly Expansion & Critical PSIR Commentary
*   **Selig S. Harrison's Thesis (*India: The Most Dangerous Decades*, 1960):**
    *   Harrison predicted that post-colonial India would disintegrate under the centrifugal pressures of regionalism, linguistic rivalries, and communal fragmentation once the towering unifying presence of Jawaharlal Nehru waned.
    *   While the 1950s witnessed the linguistic reorganization of states (States Reorganisation Act, 1956), the 1960s witnessed the sudden rupture of post-partition communal peace.
*   **The 1961 Jabalpur Riots as a Watershed:**
    *   Following the horrific violence of Partition, India experienced a relative lull in large-scale communal violence during the 1950s, largely attributed to the post-assassination delegitimization of Hindu communal organizations and Nehru’s moral stature.
    *   In 1961, the **Jabalpur Riots** shattered this complacency. Triggered by a localized interpersonal incident between an elite Muslim bidi-magnate's son and a Hindu girl, the violence rapidly took on an organized, commercial-rivalry dynamic.
    *   This was followed by brutal riots across industrial belts: **Ranchi (1967)**, **Nagpur (1968)**, **Aligarh**, and the devastating **Ahmedabad Riots of 1969** (in which over 500 people, overwhelmingly Muslims, were killed).
*   **The Urban-Industrial Political Economy of 1960s Riots:**
    *   Unlike agrarian communal conflicts, 1960s riots were concentrated in urban industrial nodes characterized by rapid migrant influx, working-class demographic competition, commercial rivalries (e.g., bidi industry in Jabalpur, power looms in Bhiwandi), and localized economic displacement.
*   **Jawaharlal Nehru's Response — The National Integration Council (NIC):**
    *   Deeply shaken by Jabalpur, Nehru convened the **National Integration Conference in 1961**, establishing the **National Integration Council (NIC)** in 1962.
    *   The NIC brought together the Prime Minister, Chief Ministers, party leaders, and public intellectuals to formulate measures to combat communalism, casteism, regionalism, and linguism.
    *   *Margin Note ("write History"):* Refers to Nehru's pedagogical imperative that communalism in India could only be neutralized through scientific temper, modern education, and rewriting Indian history to highlight syncretic composite culture (*Ganga-Jamuni Tehzeeb*).

#### 3. Scholarly Expansion & Critical PSIR Commentary
*   **Phase III: Indira Gandhi & Instrumental Communalism (1970s–1984):**
    *   *From Hegemony to Crisis of Governability:* As documented by Atul Kohli (*Democracy and Discontent: India's Growing Crisis of Governability*), the centralization and de-institutionalization of the Congress under Indira Gandhi led her regime to utilize ethno-religious symbols opportunistically.
    *   *The Punjab Tragedy:* To counteract the peasant-based Jat Sikh political dominance of the **Shiromani Akali Dal**, the central leadership fostered parallel radical factions (patronizing Jarnail Singh Bhindranwale) and allied with Scheduled Caste Sikh agricultural laborers (Mazhabi Sikhs).
    *   This instrumentalization slipped out of state control, generating armed separatism (Khalistan movement), leading to **Operation Blue Star** (June 1984), the desecration and damage to the Golden Temple complex, the assassination of Indira Gandhi (31 October 1984), and the state-complicit **1984 Anti-Sikh Pogrom** in Delhi, Kanpur, and across North India.
    *   *Rural Penetration:* Crucially, communal mobilization broke its historic urban boundary and infected agrarian heartlands.
*   **Phase IV: Rajiv Gandhi & Competitive "Double Appeasement" (1984–1989):**
    *   Faced with electoral vulnerability and conservative opposition, Rajiv Gandhi pursued a disastrous strategy of dual sectarian appeasement:
        1.  *Conservative Muslim Appeasement:* Overruled the progressive verdict in *Mohd. Ahmed Khan v. Shah Bano Begum* (1985) by enacting the **Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act, 1986**, capitulating to orthodox clerical pressure from the All India Muslim Personal Law Board (AIMPLB).
        2.  *Conservative Hindu Appeasement:* To balance Hindu backlash against the Shah Bano capitulation, the regime facilitated the unlocking of the gates of the disputed **Babri Masjid-Ram Janmabhoomi** structure in Ayodhya (1986) and permitted the **Shilanyas** (foundation-laying ceremony) in 1989.
    *   *Political Repercussions:* Rather than stabilizing secularism, this validated the critique of **"pseudo-secularism"** spearheaded by L.K. Advani and galvanized the **Vishva Hindu Parishad (VHP)** and the **Bharatiya Janata Party (BJP)** to launch a mass movement for the construction of a Ram temple.
*   **Phase V: V.P. Singh, Mandal vs. Kamandal (1989–1990):**
    *   *The Coalition Crisis:* V.P. Singh’s National Front government faced acute economic crisis, escalating insurgencies in Jammu & Kashmir and Punjab, and internal coalition sabotage by Deputy PM Devi Lal.
    *   *Mandal Announcement:* To consolidate a backward-caste subaltern social coalition (OBCs), V.P. Singh announced the implementation of the **Mandal Commission** recommendations (27% OBC reservation in Central civil services) in August 1990.
    *   *Kamandal Counter-Mobilization:* To prevent the vertical fragmentation of the Hindu electorate along caste lines, the BJP and Sangh Parivar responded with **Kamandal** politics — L.K. Advani launched the pan-Indian **Somnath to Ayodhya Rath Yatra** in September 1990 to consolidate a pan-Hindu political identity.
    *   Advani's arrest in Samastipur (Bihar) by Lalu Prasad Yadav led to the withdrawal of BJP support and the collapse of the V.P. Singh government.

#### 3. Scholarly Expansion & Critical PSIR Commentary
*   **The Chain of Major Riots Post-1990:**
    1.  *Babri Masjid Demolition (6 December 1992):* The razing of the 16th-century mosque by Kar Sevaks triggered widespread communal violence across the subcontinent.
    2.  *Bombay Riots (December 1992 – January 1993):* Investigated by the Justice B.N. Srikrishna Commission, revealing targeted violence against minorities orchestrated by organized cadres, followed by the March 1993 underworld bomb blasts.
    3.  *Godhra Train Carnage & Gujarat Riots (February–March 2002):* The burning of the S-6 coach of the Sabarmati Express at Godhra resulted in state-wide retributive pogroms against Muslims, characterized by unprecedented sexual violence, mass displacement, and administrative complicity.
    4.  *Muzaffarnagar Riots (Western UP, August–September 2013):* Clashes between Jat and Muslim agricultural communities, weaponized through fake viral videos and mahapanchayats, fracturing the historic Jat-Muslim agrarian solidarity forged by Chaudhary Charan Singh and polarizing Western UP prior to the 2014 General Elections.

*   **Paul R. Brass's Tripartite Model of Riot Production (*The Production of Hindu-Muslim Violence in Contemporary India*, 2003):**
    *   Brass conducted field investigations in Aligarh over four decades, concluding that riots are **"theatrical productions"** engineered through a distinct division of labor across three phases:
        1.  **Preparation / Rehearsal (The "Fire Tenders"):**
            *   Communal animosity is kept simmering perpetually.
            *   *Fire tenders* (local political entrepreneurs, propagandists, and extremist ideologues) maintain an atmosphere of perceived threat, historical grievance, and constant friction through provocative hate speech and rumor mongering.
        2.  **Activation / Precipitation (The "Conversion Specialists"):**
            *   When an ordinary incident occurs (a road accident, an eve-teasing complaint, an altercation between youth of different communities), *conversion specialists* deliberately transform a private secular dispute into a communal flashpoint.
            *   Sub-proletariat, unemployed youth, and lumpen elements ("lower strata") are hired and armed as foot soldiers to execute violence.
            *   Timing is almost invariably linked to electoral cycles to polarize votes.
        3.  **Explanation / Interpretation (Post-Riot Blame Game):**
            *   Once violence subsides, competing political narratives, media reports, and inquiry commissions produce an ambiguous fog of explanations.
            *   Blame is attributed to "anti-social elements", external provocateurs, or spontaneous mob frenzy, effectively shielding the political architects of the violence while enabling electoral consolidation across polarized vote banks.

#### 3. Scholarly Expansion & Critical PSIR Commentary
*   **The Conservative Muslim Dissent in the Constituent Assembly:**
    *   Muslim representatives such as **Mohamad Ismail Sahib, Mahboob Ali Beg, B. Pocker Sahib, and Naziruddin Ahmad** introduced amendments stipulating that personal laws should be exempted from state interference.
    *   They argued that personal law was inextricably linked with religious belief and practice protected under **Article 25** (freedom of conscience and religion), and that forcing a uniform civil code upon an unwilling minority would violate democratic consensus and religious autonomy.
    *   *Ambedkar’s Reconciliatory Formulation:* Dr. Ambedkar declined to make personal law an inviolable fundamental right, arguing that no religious dogma could permanently freeze personal status laws in a secular democracy. However, he offered an important pragmatic assurance:
        > *"No Government can exercise its power in such a manner as to provoke the Muslim community to rise in rebellion. I think it would be a sovereignly unwise government that would do so... the Code, when made, should apply only to those who declare that they are prepared to be bound by it."*
    *   Consequently, UCC was accommodated as a **non-justiciable Directive Principle under Article 44**:
        > *"The State shall endeavour to secure for the citizens a Uniform Civil Code throughout the territory of India."*
*   **Political Inertia and the Role of the Law Commission:**
    *   *Legislative Hesitation:* Neither Congress nor successive non-Congress regimes enacted UCC, fearing loss of minority electoral support and severe communal friction.
    *   *21st Law Commission Report (2018, Consultation Paper on Reform of Family Law):*
        *   Under Justice B.S. Chauhan, the 21st Law Commission famously concluded that a Uniform Civil Code is **"neither necessary nor desirable at this stage"**.
        *   Instead, the Commission recommended **internal gender reforms within each personal law** — codifying customs, eliminating gender-discriminatory practices (e.g., unequal inheritance shares, polygamy, unilateral divorce), and harmonizing family law protections without erasing cultural diversity.
    *   *22nd Law Commission (2023):* Re-opened the UCC debate by seeking fresh inputs from religious bodies and civil society.
*   **Feminist Mobilization from Within the Minority Community:**
    *   Crucially, the demand for reform is no longer driven solely by majoritarian or judicial rhetoric. Grassroots Muslim women's organizations have led the legal and social battle:
        1.  **Bharatiya Muslim Mahila Andolan (BMMA):** Co-founded by Zakia Soman and Noorjehan Safia Niaz. BMMA conducted nationwide surveys demonstrating that 92% of Muslim women demanded a total ban on oral unilateral triple talaq (*talaq-e-biddat*). They drafted a comprehensive model *Draft Muslim Family Law* grounded in the egalitarian principles of the Quran and the Indian Constitution.
        2.  **Aawaaz-e-Niswaan (Voice of Women):** Founded in Mumbai in 1987 in the immediate aftermath of the Shah Bano controversy, advocating for legal literacy, shelter, and gender justice for Muslim women trapped between orthodox clerical patriarchy and state neglect.
*   **Key Judicial Precedents Shaping the UCC Jurisprudence:**
    1.  *Mohd. Ahmed Khan v. Shah Bano Begum (1985):*
        *   A 73-year-old destitute Muslim woman was granted maintenance under **Section 125 of the Code of Criminal Procedure (CrPC)**.
        *   Chief Justice Y.V. Chandrachud ruled that Section 125 is a secular, universal humanitarian provision overriding personal laws. The Court famously lamented that Article 44 had remained a *"dead letter"*.
    2.  *Sarla Mudgal v. Union of India (1995) & Lily Thomas (2000):*
        *   Addressed the fraudulent practice of Hindu men converting to Islam solely to solemnize a second marriage without dissolving their first Hindu marriage, thereby evading Section 494 IPC (bigamy).
        *   Justice Kuldip Singh emphasized that non-implementation of UCC violates the rights of **both Hindu and Muslim women**, urging the Prime Minister to take a fresh look at Article 44.
    3.  *Danial Latifi v. Union of India (2001):*
        *   Challenged the constitutional validity of the *Muslim Women (Protection of Rights on Divorce) Act, 1986*.
        *   In a brilliant act of judicial craftsmanship, the Supreme Court upheld the Act by interpreting Section 3(1)(a) to mean that the former husband must provide a *"reasonable and fair provision and maintenance"* **within** the iddat period that extends to provide for the woman's **entire future life** or until she remarries.
    4.  *Shayara Bano v. Union of India (2017):*
        *   A 5-judge constitutional bench struck down *Talaq-e-Biddat* (instant triple talaq) by a 3:2 majority as **unconstitutional, arbitrary, and violative of Article 14 (Equality before Law)**, establishing that arbitrary practices are not protected as "essential religious practices" under Article 25.

## Key Terms, Scholars & Pedagogical Glossaries

*   **Institutionalised Riot System (IRS):** Concept coined by political scientist **Paul R. Brass** explaining that communal violence in India is produced by a permanent, locally institutionalized network of actors categorized as "fire tenders", "conversion specialists", and "interpreters" who execute rehearsals, precipitate riots around elections, and manufacture post-hoc narratives.
*   **Picnic Riots:** Sociological concept introduced by **Dipankar Gupta** describing modern communal riots not as symmetric warfare between two belligerent communities, but as asymmetric rampages where mobs attack defenseless minority populations with administrative complicity and police indifference, returning home unharmed as celebratory participants.
*   **Plebeianisation of Indian Democracy:** Concept articulated by **Thomas Blom Hansen** and **Christophe Jaffrelot** to designate the rise to political power of lower castes and subaltern classes (*plebeians*). Hansen argues this democratic upsurge provoked deep anxiety among upper and lower-middle classes, driving them to embrace Hindu nationalism as an ideology of order and cultural restoration.
*   **Monistic Theory of Law:** Classical legal positivism developed by **John Austin**, asserting that law is the command of an unchallengeable secular sovereign backed by sanctions. It stands in direct contrast to sociological **Legal Pluralism**, which accommodates multiple competing religious and customary legal systems within a single state.
*   **Double Appeasement:** The political strategy executed by the Rajiv Gandhi administration in 1986, simultaneously appeasing conservative Muslim clerical factions (by overturning the *Shah Bano* verdict via the 1986 Act) and conservative Hindu factions (by unlocking the Babri Masjid gates and permitting Shilanyas), which ultimately destabilized Indian secularism and catalyzed militant communal mobilization.

### Question 1:
> *"Communal riots in post-independence India are neither spontaneous outbursts of primordial hatred nor mere breakdowns of law and order; they are institutionalized political productions." Critically examine this statement with reference to the theories of Paul Brass and Ashutosh Varshney.* (20 Marks / 250 Words)

#### Answer Blueprint:
1.  **Introduction:** Define communal violence in post-colonial India; contrast the primordialist view (spontaneous ancient hatred) with modern political sociology perspectives.
2.  **Ashutosh Varshney’s Civic Engagement Thesis:** Outline the mechanism of civic networks (associational vs. everyday). Explain how inter-ethnic associations (trade unions, merchant guilds) act as institutional shock-absorbers that suppress rumors and prevent sparks from turning into fires.
3.  **Paul Brass’s Institutionalised Riot System (IRS):** Elaborate on Brass's critique of the spontaneity myth based on his field research in Aligarh. Detail the three operational phases:
    *   *Preparation:* Role of "fire tenders" in keeping tension alive.
    *   *Precipitation:* Role of "conversion specialists" and lumpen elements near election cycles.
    *   *Explanation:* Elite blame-shifting and vote-bank consolidation.
4.  **Critical Comparison & Contemporary Examples:** Cite Dipankar Gupta's "Picnic Riots"; apply the IRS framework to the 2002 Gujarat riots and 2013 Muzaffarnagar violence.
5.  **Conclusion:** Synthesize that while Varshney identifies the conditions of societal resilience, Brass unmasks the machinations of political agency; both confirm that communal riots are functional mechanisms of political mobilization rather than spontaneous religious clashes.
