## Thematic Precursor & Core Conceptual Framework: Evolution of the Indian Party System

To contextualize the handwritten notes of **Lecture 63** (which commence midway through the analysis of the decline of Congress and transition directly into the Coalition Era), the following structural matrix establishes the historical, theoretical, and institutional architecture governing Indian party politics:

### 1. Conceptualizing the Party System Trajectory
1. **Phase I: The Congress System / One-Party Dominant System (1952–1967):**
   * *Morris-Jones's Formulation:* Characterized India's system as a **"one-party dominant system"**, distinct from authoritarian one-party regimes because other parties were legally and practically free to compete, criticize, and mobilize, even though they lacked systemic capability to dislodge the ruling party.
   * *Rajni Kothari's Formulation:* Coined the classic term **"The Congress System"**. Congress operated as a vast, overarching **"Party of Consensus"** containing multiple internal ideological factions (Left, Right, Centrist). The opposition parties functioned not as alternatives to the government, but as **"Parties of Pressure"** acting on the marginal factions of Congress.
   * *Nehruvian Accommodative Consensus:* Maintained internal democratic climate, tolerated dissent, preserved organizational elections, and co-opted emerging regional satraps (e.g., K. Kamaraj in Madras, B.C. Roy in Bengal, Y.B. Chavan in Maharashtra).

2. **Phase II: Breakdown, De-institutionalisation, and Confrontation (1967–1989):**
   * *The 1967 Watershed (4th General Elections):* Congress lost power in eight major states across the Hindi heartland and South India (Tamil Nadu fell to DMK, West Bengal to United Front), forming a coalition in a ninth. This gave birth to the **Samyukta Vidhayak Dal (SVD)** coalition governments, the culture of political defections (*"Aaya Ram, Gaya Ram"*), and the first rural OBC peasant assertion (Charan Singh's Bharatiya Kranti Dal).
   * *De-institutionalisation under Indira Gandhi:* Indira Gandhi bypassed the Congress "Syndicate" (traditional organizational bosses), split the party in 1969, and substituted party organization with charismatic, personalized plebiscitary politics. Organizational elections were discontinued after 1972. Loyalty replaced merit and grass-roots mass base; regional satraps were systematically replaced by compliant nominated chief ministers, eroding the party's institutional roots.
   * *The Confrontational Dynamic:* Direct confrontation with judicial institutions, the opposition (JP Movement 1974–75), federal state governments, and the declaration of the National Emergency (1975–1977).

3. **Phase III: Multi-Party System and Coalition Governance (1989–2014):**
   * *The 1989 Fracture:* The end of single-party parliamentary majorities. The rise of coalition governance at the Centre through three micro-phases:
     1. *Phase of Instability (1989–1999):* National Front (V.P. Singh, Chandrashekhar), United Front (H.D. Deve Gowda, I.K. Gujral), and short-lived BJP governments (13 days, 13 months). Characterized by unstable outside support from Congress or Left.
     2. *Phase of Bipolar / Bi-nodal Maturity (1999–2014):* Institutionalization of pre-poll alliances anchored around two national poles: the **National Democratic Alliance (NDA)** led by Atal Bihari Vajpayee (1999–2004) and the **United Progressive Alliance (UPA)** led by Manmohan Singh / Sonia Gandhi (2004–2014: UPA-I and UPA-II).
   * *The Federalisation of Governance:* Regional parties became kingmakers at the Centre, extracting ministerial portfolios, special financial packages, and veto power over economic and foreign policies.

4. **Phase IV: The Post-2014 Realignment — A "Second Dominant Party System"?**
   * *The 2014 Critical Juncture:* For the first time in 30 years, a single party (BJP under Narendra Modi) won an absolute majority (282 seats; NDA 336 seats), repeated with an expanded majority in 2019 (303 seats; NDA 353 seats).
   * *The Demise vs. Decline Debate:* Scholars like **Suhas Palshikar** and **E. Sridharan** argue that 2014 does not merely represent a regular electoral defeat for Congress, but the structural **demise of the Congress System** and the emergence of a **BJP-centric dominant party system** ("Fourth Party System").

#### Why 2014 Marks the "Demise" rather than Mere "Decline"
Palshikar argues that prior to 2014, even when Congress lost elections (e.g., 1977, 1989, 1996, 1998, 1999), it remained the **central pole and default gravitational axis** of national politics. The entire party system was defined either as "Pro-Congress" or "Anti-Congress". 

In 2014, however:
1. **Loss of National Hegemonic Status:** Congress was reduced to 44 seats and 19.3% votes, winning zero seats in several major states (Gujarat, Rajasthan, Delhi, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Jharkhand).
2. **Double Shrinkage:** 
   * *Social Base Shrinkage:* Complete alienation of Upper Castes, OBCs, Dalits, and youth.
   * *Territorial Shrinkage:* Loss of traditional strongholds in western and northern India (Maharashtra, Haryana, Delhi), reducing Congress to a regionalized presence in southern pockets and sporadic alliance dependencies.
3. **Emergence of the "Second Dominant Party System":** Under Narendra Modi, the BJP replaced Congress as the central systemic sun around which all other parties now revolve—ushering in an era of **BJP Hegemony** anchored on nationalist ideology, welfarism, and supreme organizational mobilization.

### Deep Dive 2: E. Sridharan's Seven-Point Model of Regionalisation

Political scientist **E. Sridharan** demonstrates that the rise of regional parties was neither accidental nor purely opportunistic, but the direct institutional outcome of seven structural transformations:

1. **Electoral Decoupling (1971):** Breaking simultaneous elections separated local issues and assembly anti-incumbency from national parliamentary mandates.
2. **Social Cleavage Politicisation:** Deepening identity consciousness converted caste, linguistic, and cultural differences into durable vote banks.
3. **Agrarian Economic Shift:** Land reforms (zamindari abolition) and the Green Revolution generated a wealthy surplus-producing intermediate peasantry (Jats, Yadavs, Marathas, Patidars) seeking state power to secure subsidies, procurement prices, and administrative influence.
4. **Emergency Backlash (1975–77):** Resistance to Indira Gandhi's authoritarian centralization forged cross-caste, regional anti-centralist coalitions that became permanent regional parties.
5. **Constitutional Functionalism:** The Indian Constitution allocates citizen-centric governance (law, order, land, agriculture, welfare) to the State List (List II), making the state government the decisive patron of voter livelihoods.
6. **Instrumentalization of Religion:** Polarizing national cultural politics spurred regional counter-mobilizations protecting regional distinctiveness.
7. **Continental Diversity:** In a subcontinent of 1.4 billion people speaking 22 scheduled languages with thousands of caste divisions, a single centrist umbrella party was sociologically unsustainable once democratic consciousness matured.

### Deep Dive 3: Coalition Theory: William H. Riker vs. Indian Realities & Vidyut Chakrabarty's Analysis

#### 1. Riker's Theory of Political Coalitions (1962)
American political theorist **William H. Riker** formulated the famous **"Size Principle"**:
$$\text{Size Principle: } \text{In zero-sum situations, participants create coalitions just as large as they believe will ensure winning, and no larger.}$$
* **Minimum Winning Coalition (MWC):** Rational actors form an alliance with exactly 50% + 1 seat to avoid sharing cabinet portfolios, discretionary resources, and patronage with unnecessary partners.

#### 2. The Indian Paradox: "Oversized Coalitions"
In India, prime ministers deliberately violate the MWC model by creating **Oversized / Surplus Coalitions**:
* *Vajpayee's NDA (1999):* Accommodated over 24 parties even when a narrower coalition could cross 272 seats.
* *Manmohan Singh's UPA-I (2004):* Assembled 218 seats in pre-poll alliance + outside support from 60 Left Front MPs + support from SP and BSP.
* *Modi's NDA (2014 & 2019):* Retained alliance partners in cabinet despite BJP possessing an absolute single-party majority.
* *Reason:* **Blackmail Insurance.** In a fragmented polity plagued by shifting loyalties, prime ministers need a numerical cushion against sudden walkouts by volatile regional satraps.

#### 3. Vidyut Chakrabarty: Western Design vs. Indian Calculation
Prof. **Vidyut Chakrabarty** demonstrates that Western coalitions (Germany, Netherlands, Scandinavia) are:
* **"Coalitions by Design":** Based on institutionalized, programmatic, and ideological commonalities negotiated over months through binding policy whitepapers.
* **Indian "Coalitions by Calculation":** Driven by post-poll opportunistic arithmetic, desperate bids to capture power, or negative pacts to keep a common enemy out of office (e.g., 1977 Janata Party uniting Socialists and Jan Sangh; 1989 National Front supported by BJP and Left). Indian coalitions remain **"in search of design"**, relying on ad-hoc arrangements rather than systemic institutional norms.

### Deep Dive 4: The Presidential vs. Parliamentary Debate in India

Whenever coalition governance produced policy paralysis, instability, or political corruption, voices from the corporate elite and political class (e.g., Shashi Tharoor, L.K. Advani during the 1990s) argued that India should switch to an **American-style Presidential System** to ensure stability.

#### 1. The Constituent Assembly Consensus: Dr. B.R. Ambedkar's Verdict
Dr. B.R. Ambedkar explicitly addressed this question in the Constituent Assembly on **November 4, 1948**:
> *"A democratic executive must satisfy two conditions: it must be a stable executive and it must be a responsible executive. Unfortunately, it has not been possible so far to devise a system which can ensure both in equal degree... The American and Swiss systems give more stability but less responsibility. The British system gives more responsibility but less stability. The Draft Constitution in recommending the Parliamentary system of Executive has preferred more responsibility to more stability."*
* India, as an infant democracy emerging from colonial subjugation, could not afford an unaccountable executive for a fixed four-year term. Daily parliamentary scrutiny through Question Hour, adjournment motions, debates, and no-confidence motions was essential to hold power accountable.

#### 2. Juan Linz: "The Perils of Presidentialism" (1990)
Political scientist **Juan Linz** demonstrated why the presidential model is catastrophic for deeply divided, multi-ethnic, developing societies:
1. **Zero-Sum / Winner-Take-All Logic:** In a presidential election, the winning candidate captures 100% of executive power with 51% (or a plurality) of votes, completely shutting out minorities and regional groups from power for a fixed term, breeding alienation and rebellion.
2. **Dual Democratic Legitimacy:** Both the President and the Legislature claim direct democratic mandates from the people. When they disagree—especially during divided government—there is no constitutional mechanism to resolve the deadlock, frequently leading to constitutional crisis and military intervention.
3. **Rigidity of Fixed Tenure:** A corrupt, incompetent, or authoritarian president cannot be easily removed except through the near-impossible threshold of impeachment.
4. **Vulnerability to Authoritarianism:** Fosters a savior complex / Caesarism, where the president claims to personally embody the "will of the nation", marginalizing democratic institutions.

#### 3. The Constitutional Impossibility: Basic Structure Doctrine
Even if Parliament wished to switch to a presidential system, the Supreme Court has made it constitutionally impossible:
* *Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala (1973):* Established that Parliament's constituent power under Article 368 cannot alter the **Basic Structure** of the Constitution.
* *Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain (1975) & S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994):* Explicitly recognized the **Parliamentary System of Democracy** and the **separation of powers with executive accountability to the legislature** as core, non-negotiable facets of the Basic Structure.
