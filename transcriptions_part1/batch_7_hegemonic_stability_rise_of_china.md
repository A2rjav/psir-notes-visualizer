### Hegemonic Stability Theory: The Provision of Global Public Goods
- **Archival Reference:** `page_086.png` to `page_088.png` (Kindleberger & Keohane Frameworks).
- **Core Premise of Hegemonic Stability Theory (HST):** The international system is inherently anarchic. An open, liberal, stable global economic and political order requires the leadership of a single dominant state — a **Hegemon** — possessing both the overwhelming material capability and the political will to enforce international rules.
- **Charles Kindleberger (*The World in Depression 1929–1939*, 1973):**
  - Argued that the catastrophic Great Depression of 1929 was caused by a hegemonic transition void: Great Britain was *unable* to stabilize the global financial system, and the rising power, the United States, was *unwilling* (isolationism).
  - *"For the world economy to be stabilized, there must be a stabilizer, one stabilizer."*
- **Five Essential Global Public Goods Provided by a Hegemon:**
  1. An open and stable market for distressed goods.
  2. A continuous, counter-cyclical flow of capital.
  3. A stable international currency system (Reserve currency / US Dollar).
  4. Coordination of macroeconomic policies.
  5. Maintaining freedom of the seas (open maritime SLOCs) and global security enforcement.
- **Robert Keohane's Institutionalist Revision (*After Hegemony*, 1984):** Hegemonic power may decline, but international institutions and regimes (IMF, World Bank, WTO, UN) create mutual transparency and reduce transaction costs, allowing international cooperation to persist even after the hegemon's dominance wanes.

### The Rise of China and the Thucydides Trap
- **Archival Reference:** `page_088.png` to `page_091.png` (Thucydides Trap & Allison's Thesis).
- **Graham Allison's Seminal Concept (*Destined for War: Can America and China Escape Thucydides's Trap?*, 2017):**
  - Derived from ancient Greek historian Thucydides's analysis of the Peloponnesian War:
  - *"It was the rise of Athens and the fear that this inspired in Sparta that made war inevitable."*
  - **The Thucydides Trap:** The severe structural stress that occurs when a rising power (e.g., China) threatens to displace an established, ruling hegemon (e.g., the United States).
  - Allison analyzed 16 historical cases of hegemonic rivalry over the past 500 years; 12 ended in catastrophic catastrophic war (e.g., World War I between rising Germany and ruling Britain).
- **China's Shift under Xi Jinping:** Moving away from Deng Xiaoping's cautious foreign policy dictum (*Taoguang Yanghui* — "Hide your strength, bide your time"), Xi proclaimed the **"Chinese Dream"** (*Zhongguo Meng*) of national rejuvenation by 2049, asserting revisionist claims in the South China Sea, East China Sea, and the Himalayan border with India.

### John Mearsheimer's Offensive Realism: The Inevitability of Conflict
- **Archival Reference:** `page_091.png` to `page_093.png` (Offensive Realism & The Tragedy of Great Power Politics).
- **Five Core Assumptions of Offensive Realism:**
  1. The international system is anarchic.
  2. Great powers inherently possess offensive military capability.
  3. States can never be completely certain of other states' future intentions.
  4. Survival is the primary driving goal of great powers.
  5. Great powers are rational actors.
- **Can China Rise Peacefully? Mearsheimer's Resounding "NO":**
  - If China continues its economic growth, it will translate economic wealth into overwhelming military might, seeking to establish **regional hegemony in Asia** just as the United States established hegemony in the Western Hemisphere through the Monroe Doctrine.
  - China will seek to push the US military out of Asia (Anti-Access/Area-Denial / A2/AD) and dictate terms to neighbors.
  - The United States and its Asian regional partners (India, Japan, Australia, South Korea) will inevitably form a balancing coalition to contain Chinese hegemony, making intense geopolitical competition and potential war structurally unavoidable.

### Samuel Huntington's Clash of Civilizations in the Asian Context
- **Archival Reference:** `page_093.png` to `page_096.png` (Huntington's Clash of Civilizations, 1993/1996).
- **Post-Cold War Fault Lines:** With the collapse of ideological divisions (Capitalism vs Communism), the fundamental source of global conflict is cultural and civilizational identity.
- **The World Divided into Nine Civilizations:** Western, Latin American, African, Islamic, Sinic (Confucian), Hindu, Orthodox, Buddhist, and Japanese.
- **Huntington's Specific Predictions for Asia:**
  - The resurgence of Sinic-Confucian civilization challenging Western global dominance.
  - Potential alliance between Islamic and Confucian civilizations against Western hegemony.
  - Hindu civilization (India) acting as a pivotal **"Swing State"** — retaining autonomous cultural identity while tactically aligning with Western or Asian civilizations based on balance of power calculations.
- **S. Jaishankar's Concept of "Multipolar Asia":** "An Asian Century cannot exist without a Multipolar Asia." India firmly rejects a unipolar Asian hegemony dominated by Beijing, insisting on genuine multipolarity.

### Active Recall Flashcards (Batch 7)
- **Q1:** What is the core argument of Charles Kindleberger's Hegemonic Stability Theory?
  **A1:** That an open and stable global economic order requires a single dominant hegemon willing and able to provide international public goods (free seas, stable currency, crisis lending).
- **Q2:** What is Graham Allison's "Thucydides Trap"?
  **A2:** The dangerous structural stress and propensity toward war that arises when a rising power threatens to displace an established, ruling hegemon, historically illustrated by Athens and Sparta.
- **Q3:** Why does John Mearsheimer argue that China cannot rise peacefully?
  **A3:** Under offensive realism, great powers maximize their security by seeking regional hegemony; China will naturally seek to dominate Asia and expel US power, triggering an inevitable balancing containment coalition.
