### Hobbes's Theory of Sovereignty: Absolute, Indivisible and Inalienable
- **Archival Reference:** `page_073.png` to `page_075.png` (Leviathan, Chapter 18 & 21).
- **Absolute Attributes of Hobbesian Sovereignty:**
  1. *Indivisibility:* Sovereign power cannot be divided between branches (Crown, Parliament, Judiciary) or shared with religious institutions. Dividing sovereignty is to destroy it: "A kingdom divided against itself cannot stand."
  2. *Inalienability:* The sovereign cannot transfer, abdicate, or forfeit power without dissolving the state back into the brutal state of nature.
  3. *Unconditional Law-Making Authority (Legal Positivism):* Law is strictly "the command of him or them that have the sovereign power." There is no higher natural law or custom that can invalidate a sovereign statute.
- **Control over Opinions and Religious Doctrine:** The Sovereign has absolute authority to judge which doctrines and religious opinions are conducive to peace, subordinate the church to the state (Erastianism), and appoint all ministers and magistrates.
- **Liberty of Subjects:** Liberty in Hobbes is purely **negative** — the absence of external physical impediments. Where the sovereign has not enacted a law, subjects possess complete liberty to act as they see fit (the "Silence of the Law").

### The Paradox of Hobbes: Absolute Sovereignty Founded on Radical Individualism
- **Archival Reference:** `page_075.png` to `page_077.png` (Hobbesian Individualism & The Right to Resistance).
- **Hobbes as the Greatest Individualist:** As Michael Oakeshott and C.E. Vaughan emphasize, Hobbes's political philosophy starts not with the divine right of kings, the organic polis, or traditional community, but with the **autonomous, isolated, self-interested individual**.
- **Utilitarian Justification of the State:** The state is not a sacred entity; it is a purely artificial, mechanical instrument (*machina*) constructed by individuals solely for their personal utility and security.
- **The Inalienable Right to Life (The Limit of Obligation):**
  - An individual surrenders rights to the Leviathan for the sole purpose of preserving their life. Therefore, the **Right to Self-Preservation can never be surrendered**.
  - If the Sovereign commands an individual to kill himself, wound himself, or submit to immediate execution without trial, the individual retains the natural right to resist.
  - If the Sovereign is captured by enemies and loses the power to protect its subjects, the obligation of obedience dissolves, and individuals return to the state of nature.
- **C.B. Macpherson's Thesis in *The Political Theory of Possessive Individualism* (1962):** Hobbes's atomized individual reflects the competitive, market-driven psychology of early modern capitalism, where the individual owes nothing to society.

### Historical Transition to John Locke: The Glorious Revolution of 1688
- **Archival Reference:** `page_078.png` to `page_080.png` (Context of 1688 & Two Treatises).
- **John Locke (1632–1704):** The intellectual architect of modern Constitutionalism, Classical Liberalism, and Natural Rights theory.
- **The Glorious Revolution (1688):** Bloodless overthrow of King James II, accession of William and Mary, and enactment of the English Bill of Rights (1689), establishing Parliamentary supremacy and constitutional monarchy.
- **Peter Laslett's Scholarly Reinterpretation:** Laslett demonstrated that Locke wrote the *Two Treatises of Government* not merely *ex post facto* to justify the Glorious Revolution of 1688, but earlier (1679–1681) during the Exclusion Crisis to justify revolutionary resistance against Stuart absolutism.

### Locke's First Treatise: Demolition of Sir Robert Filmer's *Patriarcha*
- **Archival Reference:** `page_080.png` to `page_082.png` (Refutation of Patriarchal Divine Right).
- **Sir Robert Filmer's Thesis in *Patriarcha* (1680):** Filmer defended absolute royal divine right through biblical patriarchy. God granted absolute paternal dominion over the earth and all humans to Adam; kings are the direct linear heirs of Adam's patriarchal authority. Citizens are born into natural servitude to their king, just as children are born into obedience to their fathers.
- **Locke's Systematic Refutation in the *First Treatise*:**
  1. God gave the earth to Adam in common with all mankind, not as private exclusive dominion.
  2. Adam possessed parental authority to nurture children, not absolute power of life and death over adults.
  3. Paternal authority belongs equally to the Mother, not solely to the Father ("Honor thy father and mother").
  4. Even if Adam had monarchical authority, his direct heir cannot be historically traced; therefore, no modern king can claim legitimate title to rule based on Adam's inheritance.
- **Significance:** By demolishing Filmer's divine patriarchal authority, Locke cleared the intellectual ground for his *Second Treatise*, demonstrating that legitimate political power can only originate from the **voluntary consent of free and equal individuals**.

### Locke's Second Treatise: Human Nature and Epistemology
- **Archival Reference:** `page_082.png` to `page_084.png` (Second Treatise & Human Nature).
- **Epistemology of *An Essay Concerning Human Understanding* (1689):** The human mind at birth is a blank slate (*Tabula Rasa*). All knowledge derives from sensory experience and reflection.
- **Human Nature in Locke:** In stark contrast to Hobbes's predatory beasts, Locke views human nature as inherently **rational, moral, sociable, and cooperative**. Humans are endowed with natural reason, which teaches them that being all equal and independent, no one ought to harm another in his life, health, liberty, or possessions.

### Active Recall Flashcards (Batch 7)
- **Q1:** Under what specific condition does a Hobbesian subject retain the right to disobey the Sovereign?
  **A1:** When the Sovereign commands an individual to kill, maim, or forfeit his own life, or when the Sovereign completely loses the coercive ability to protect the subject's life.
- **Q2:** What was Sir Robert Filmer's central thesis in *Patriarcha*, and why did Locke refute it?
  **A2:** Filmer argued that kings possess absolute divine power inherited from Adam's patriarchal dominion. Locke refuted it to prove that political authority is not divinely or parentally inherited, but grounded solely in the voluntary consent of free and equal individuals.
- **Q3:** How does Locke's conception of human nature differ fundamentally from Hobbes's?
  **A3:** Hobbes views human nature as egoistic, fearful, and perpetually competing for power, whereas Locke views humans as rational, sociable, moral agents guided by the Law of Nature.

### Model Answer: The Paradox of Hobbesian Individualism (20 Marks)
- **Question:** "Hobbes is the greatest of all individualists." Critically evaluate this statement, demonstrating how an authoritarian Leviathan is erected upon radical individualist foundations.
- **Structure:**
  - **Introduction:** Present the paradox: Hobbes is commonly remembered as an apologist for absolute despotism, yet historical scholars (Oakeshott, Macpherson) identify him as the founder of modern political individualism.
  - **Body Paragraph 1:** Trace the individualist premise: mechanical materialism, individual appetites, absence of natural teleological community, and the foundational equality of individuals in the state of nature.
  - **Body Paragraph 2:** Analyze the contract as an instrumental, utilitarian creation: individuals construct the Leviathan purely for self-preservation and personal utility, not out of spiritual reverence.
  - **Body Paragraph 3:** Examine the boundaries of political obligation: the inalienability of the Right to Self-Preservation, proving the state remains an instrument subservient to individual life.
  - **Critical Evaluation:** Weigh Macpherson's critique of possessive market individualism against the critique that Hobbes ultimately sacrifices political liberty and constitutional rights on the altar of absolute sovereign order.
  - **Conclusion:** Conclude that Hobbesian absolutism is uniquely modern because it derives the unlimited power of the state exclusively from the rational consent of atomic individuals.
