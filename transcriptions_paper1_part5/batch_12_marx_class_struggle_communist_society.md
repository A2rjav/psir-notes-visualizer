### The Epochs of Human History and the Theory of Class Struggle
- **Archival Reference:** `page_133.png` to `page_135.png` (Historical Epochs & Manifesto).
- **The Immortal Opening of *The Communist Manifesto* (1848):**
  - *"The history of all hitherto existing society is the history of class struggle."*
  - Freeman and slave, patrician and plebeian, lord and serf, guild-master and journeyman — in a word, oppressor and oppressed, stood in constant opposition to one another, carrying on an uninterrupted fight that each time ended either in a revolutionary reconstitution of society at large, or in the common ruin of the contending classes.
- **Four Historical Epochs of Production:**
  1. *Primitive Communism:* Absence of private property; hunting and gathering; no classes, no exploitation, and no state.
  2. *Ancient Slave Society:* Primary contradiction: Masters vs Slaves (Slaves owned directly as physical chattel).
  3. *Feudal Society:* Primary contradiction: Feudal Landlords vs Serfs (Serfs tied to the land, surrendering agrarian surplus).
  4. *Capitalist Society:* Primary contradiction: Bourgeoisie (owners of capital/means of production) vs Proletariat (wage labourers owning only their labour-power).
- **Class Polarization under Capitalism:** Unlike complex medieval feudal estates, modern capitalist society simplifies class antagonisms into two vast hostile camps directly facing each other: **Bourgeoisie and Proletariat**. The intermediate petty-bourgeoisie and artisans are relentlessly squeezed out into the wage-earning proletariat.

### The Economic Architecture of *Das Kapital*: Labour Theory of Value and Surplus Value
- **Archival Reference:** `page_135.png` to `page_138.png` (Das Kapital, Volume 1: Surplus Value).
- **Use-Value vs Exchange-Value:**
  - *Use-Value:* The qualitative utility of a commodity to satisfy human needs.
  - *Exchange-Value:* The quantitative proportion in which a commodity exchanges for other commodities in the market.
- **The Labour Theory of Value:** The exchange-value of any commodity is determined objectively by the amount of **Socially Necessary Labour Time** required for its production.
- **The Unique Commodity: Labour-Power:** The worker does not sell "labour"; he sells his capacity to work (**Labour-Power**). The value of labour-power is determined by the cost of the basic necessities required to keep the worker and his family alive and able to work another day (subsistence wage).
- **Theory of Surplus Value (*Mehrwert*):**
  - Suppose a worker requires 4 hours of labour to reproduce the value of his daily subsistence wage. The capitalist forces him to work for 10 hours.
  - The first 4 hours represent **Necessary Labour** (paid to the worker as wages).
  - The remaining 6 hours represent **Surplus Labour**, which crystallizes into **Surplus Value** — expropriated entirely by the capitalist as unearned profit.
- **Two Forms of Surplus Value Extraction:**
  1. *Absolute Surplus Value:* Prolonging the length of the working day (e.g., from 10 to 14 hours).
  2. *Relative Surplus Value:* Increasing technological productivity through mechanization, reducing the necessary labour time required to produce subsistence goods, thereby expanding the surplus share.

### Internal Contradictions and the Inevitable Collapse of Capitalism
- **Archival Reference:** `page_138.png` to `page_140.png` (Contradictions & Crises of Capitalism).
- **The Law of the Tendency of the Rate of Profit to Fall:**
  - Driven by cutthroat market competition, capitalists continuously invest in machinery (**Constant Capital, c**) to replace human workers (**Variable Capital, v**).
  - The Organic Composition of Capital (*c / v*) rises inexorably.
  - Since surplus value can only be extracted from living human labour (*v*), reducing the proportion of human workers inevitably drives down the overall rate of profit across the capitalist economy.
- **Crisis of Overproduction / Underconsumption:** Modern industrial technology creates unprecedented productive abundance, but because workers are paid bare subsistence wages, they lack the purchasing power to buy the commodities produced. Warehouses fill up, factories shutter, unemployment spikes, and systemic crises ravage the economy.
- **Class-in-itself (*Klasse an sich*) to Class-for-itself (*Klasse für sich*):**
  - *Class-in-itself:* Proletarians share the same objective socioeconomic position, but remain fragmented, competing against one another for survival wages, suffering from false consciousness.
  - *Class-for-itself:* Through common workplace concentration, union struggles, and communist vanguard agitation, workers achieve subjective revolutionary class consciousness, recognizing their historic mission to abolish capitalism.

### The Marxist Theory of the State: The Instrument of Class Rule
- **Archival Reference:** `page_140.png` to `page_142.png` (The State as Class Instrument).
- **The State is Not a Neutral Arbiter:** Marx refutes liberal contract theorists who view the state as a neutral umpire reconciling social interests.
- **Instrumentalist Conception of the State:** In *The Communist Manifesto*, Marx and Engels declare:
  - *"The executive of the modern state is but a committee for managing the common affairs of the whole bourgeoisie."*
  - The state is an organized coercive apparatus created by the economically dominant class to suppress the exploited class and legalistically protect private property.
- **Relative Autonomy of the State (*The 18th Brumaire of Louis Bonaparte*, 1852):**
  - In exceptional historical moments of class stalemate (where the bourgeoisie has lost the ability to rule directly, and the proletariat has not yet gained it), the state executive apparatus (Bonapartist bureaucracy and military) can achieve temporary **relative autonomy**, ruling above all social classes while fundamentally preserving the capitalist social order.

### The Stages of Communist Revolution: Socialism to Full Communism
- **Archival Reference:** `page_142.png` to `page_144.png` (Critique of the Gotha Programme, 1875).
- **The Proletarian Revolution:** Capitalism produces its own gravediggers. The immiserated and conscious working class overthrows the bourgeois state through violent revolution.
- **Stage 1: The Dictatorship of the Proletariat (Socialism):**
  - A transitional phase where the working class seizes state power to suppress counter-revolutionary bourgeois resistance, abolish private ownership of the means of production, and nationalize banking and industry.
  - Distribution Principle: *"From each according to his ability, to each according to his work."*
- **Stage 2: Full Communist Society (The Stateless and Classless Utopia):**
  - With the complete abolition of private property and class divisions, the state loses its coercive social purpose:
  - *"The government of persons is replaced by the administration of things... The state is not abolished, **it withers away**."* (Friedrich Engels, *Anti-Dühring*).
  - Distribution Principle: *"From each according to his ability, to each according to his needs!"*
  - Realization of absolute human emancipation: division of labour is overcome, enabling the multidimensional human being "to hunt in the morning, fish in the afternoon, rear cattle in the evening, criticize after dinner, without ever becoming hunter, fisherman, herdsman, or critic."

### Active Recall Flashcards (Batch 12)
- **Q1:** How does Marx define Surplus Value in *Das Kapital*?
  **A1:** Surplus value is the value produced by the worker during the surplus labour hours beyond the necessary labour time required to reproduce the value of his subsistence wage, expropriated entirely by the capitalist as profit.
- **Q2:** What is the difference between a "Class-in-itself" and a "Class-for-itself"?
  **A2:** A Class-in-itself is a group sharing the same objective socioeconomic position without awareness; a Class-for-itself possesses subjective revolutionary class consciousness and acts collectively to transform society.
- **Q3:** What is the famous distribution principle of higher, full communism in the *Critique of the Gotha Programme*?
  **A3:** "From each according to his ability, to each according to his needs!"
