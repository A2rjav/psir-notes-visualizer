### The Utilitarian Background: Jeremy Bentham's Quantitative Hedonism
- **Archival Reference:** `page_109.png` to `page_111.png` (Bentham's Utilitarian Principles).
- **Jeremy Bentham (1748–1832):** Founder of Utilitarianism. Declared nature has placed mankind under the governance of two sovereign masters: **Pain and Pleasure**.
- **Principle of Utility:** The greatest happiness of the greatest number is the measure of right and wrong.
- **Quantitative Hedonism:** Pleasures differ only in **quantity** (intensity, duration, certainty, propinquity, fecundity, purity, extent — measured by the *Felicific Calculus*), not in quality.
- **Bentham's Famous Axiom:** "Quantity of pleasure being equal, pushpin is as good as poetry."
- **Rejection of Natural Rights:** Bentham dismissed natural rights as "nonsense upon stilts" — rights can only exist through positive law enacted by the state.

### J.S. Mill's Revision of Utilitarianism: Qualitative Pleasures and Human Dignity
- **Archival Reference:** `page_111.png` to `page_113.png` (Mill's Moral Reformation of Utilitarianism).
- **John Stuart Mill (1806–1873):** Rigorously educated by James Mill and Bentham. Underwent a severe mental crisis at age 20, realizing that cold, calculating Benthamite utilitarianism ignored the human soul, poetry, and character.
- **Qualitative Distinction in Pleasures:** Mill introduced a qualitative hierarchy: intellectual, moral, and aesthetic pleasures are inherently superior to mere bodily physical gratifications.
- **Famous Axiom in *Utilitarianism* (1861):**
  - *"It is better to be a human being dissatisfied than a pig satisfied; better to be Socrates dissatisfied than a fool satisfied. And if the fool, or the pig, are of a different opinion, it is because they only know their own side of the question."*
- **Re-evaluating Mill's Revision:** By introducing quality, human dignity, and justice as independent criteria, Mill effectively demolished Bentham's mathematical felicific calculus, leading critics to remark that Mill was an "inconsistent utilitarian" but a great humanist.

### J.S. Mill's *On Liberty* (1859): Defending Individuality and the Harm Principle
- **Archival Reference:** `page_113.png` to `page_116.png` (On Liberty & Harm Principle).
- **The Threat of the "Tyranny of the Majority":** Mill warned that in modern democratic societies, the greatest threat to liberty is no longer monarchical despotism, but the social tyranny of public opinion, collective mediocrity, and societal conformity that smothers individuality.
- **The Harm Principle (The Single Foundational Criterion):**
  - *"The sole end for which mankind are warranted, individually or collectively, in interfering with the liberty of action of any of their number, is self-protection. That the only purpose for which power can be rightfully exercised over any member of a civilized community, against his will, is to prevent harm to others."*
- **Two Spheres of Human Action:**
  1. *Self-Regarding Actions:* Actions that affect only the individual agent (e.g., private reading, personal diet, religious belief). The state and society have **zero right to interfere**, even if the action is foolish or self-destructive. "Over himself, over his own body and mind, the individual is sovereign."
  2. *Other-Regarding Actions:* Actions that directly infringe upon the vital interests or rights of others (e.g., violence, theft, public disturbance). Here, society and state may legitimately intervene through law.
- **Exceptions to the Principle of Liberty:** Mill explicitly stated that the doctrine of liberty applies only to mature human beings in the maturity of their faculties. It excludes: (1) children and minors, and (2) "backward states of society" (barbarians), where paternalistic imperial rule is justified until they achieve capacity for self-government.

### Absolute Defense of Freedom of Speech and Expression
- **Archival Reference:** `page_116.png` to `page_118.png` (On Liberty, Chapter 2).
- **Mill's Famous Defense of the Dissident:**
  - *"If all mankind minus one were of one opinion, and only one person were of the contrary opinion, mankind would be no more justified in silencing that one person, than he, if he had the power, would be justified in silencing mankind."*
- **Four Classical Arguments for Free Speech:**
  1. *The Silenced Opinion May be True:* Silencing an opinion assumes one's own infallibility (e.g., the execution of Socrates and Jesus Christ by well-meaning judges who thought they were eradicating evil).
  2. *The Silenced Opinion May Contain a Portion of Truth:* Most prevailing opinions are only half-truths; the clash of opposing ideas is necessary to reveal the whole truth.
  3. *Even if Completely False, Free Speech Prevents Truth from Becoming Dead Dogma:* Unless vigorously contested, true beliefs degenerate into unthinking prejudice and dead dogma without living conviction.
  4. *Vital Meaning is Lost without Debate:* The meaning of the doctrine itself will be in danger of being lost or enfeebled, deprived of its vital effect on character and conduct.

### Mill on Representative Government and the Subjection of Women
- **Archival Reference:** `page_118.png` to `page_120.png` (Representative Government & Women).
- **Mill as a "Reluctant Democrat" (C.L. Wayper):**
  - While praising representative democracy as the best form of government for moral cultivation, Mill deeply feared the rise of uneducated working-class majorities enforcing collective mediocrity.
  - Proposed Electoral Safeguards:
    1. *Plural Voting:* Educated citizens and professionals should receive multiple votes (2, 3, or more) based on educational attainment, preventing the uneducated from overwhelming wisdom.
    2. *Open Ballot:* Rejection of secret ballot to ensure citizens vote publicly for the public interest, not private self-interest.
    3. *Proportional Representation:* Endorsed Thomas Hare's system to guarantee parliamentary representation for intellectual minorities.
- ***The Subjection of Women* (1869):** Co-authored with his companion and wife Harriet Taylor.
  - The first major feminist treatise by a canonical male Western political philosopher.
  - Denounced the legal, economic, and marital subordination of women as a barbaric relic of ancient rule by brute force, incompatible with modern liberal civilization.
  - Demanded equal educational access, property ownership, divorce rights, and women's suffrage.

### Active Recall Flashcards (Batch 10)
- **Q1:** How did J.S. Mill revise Bentham's quantitative utilitarianism?
  **A1:** Mill introduced qualitative distinctions among pleasures, asserting that intellectual and moral pleasures are inherently superior to mere bodily physical gratifications ("better to be Socrates dissatisfied than a pig satisfied").
- **Q2:** What is the core definition of the Harm Principle in *On Liberty*?
  **A2:** The only justification for state or societal interference with an individual's liberty against their will is to prevent direct harm to others; over actions affecting only himself, the individual is sovereign.
- **Q3:** Why did C.L. Wayper describe J.S. Mill as a "reluctant democrat"?
  **A3:** Because while Mill favored representative democracy, he feared the tyranny of the uneducated majority and collective mediocrity, leading him to advocate plural voting for the educated and proportional representation.

### Model Answer: J.S. Mill's Defense of Liberty and Freedom of Expression (20 Marks)
- **Question:** "If all mankind minus one were of one opinion, and only one person were of the contrary opinion, mankind would be no more justified in silencing that one person, than he... would be justified in silencing mankind." In the light of this quote, evaluate Mill's defense of freedom of speech and examine his Harm Principle.
- **Structure:**
  - **Introduction:** Situate Mill's *On Liberty* as the classic philosophical defense of individual autonomy against both political despotism and societal conformism.
  - **Body Paragraph 1:** Analyze Mill's four epistemological arguments for absolute free speech: fallibility of human judgment, the partial truth of dissident views, prevention of truth becoming dead dogma, and preservation of vital doctrinal meaning.
  - **Body Paragraph 2:** Deconstruct the Harm Principle and the division between Self-Regarding and Other-Regarding actions, emphasizing the sovereignty of the individual over their own body and mind.
  - **Body Paragraph 3:** Evaluate Mill's caveats: paternalism towards children and uncivilized societies, alongside the practical difficulties of defining purely self-regarding actions in modern interconnected society.
  - **Critical Evaluation:** Engage Isaiah Berlin's conceptualization of negative versus positive liberty, and modern critiques regarding hate speech and digital misinformation.
  - **Conclusion:** Affirm that Mill's passionate defense of cognitive freedom and eccentric individuality remains the indispensable cornerstone of modern constitutional democracy.
